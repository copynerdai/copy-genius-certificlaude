# Emotional Intelligence

> Atomic reference for dimensionalizing emotions in direct response copy. Each entry describes how an emotion manifests across three dimensions: **physical** (body language and behavior), **mental** (thoughts and cognitive patterns), **visceral** (internal bodily sensations). Writing specialists consult this file at key moments and **regenerate** the manifestation as natural-language scene adapted to the specific character, context, and brand voice of the copy.
>
> **This database is the CANONICAL emotion taxonomy of the system: specialists label emotions using these entry names.**

---

## How to Use This File

Anchors in briefs always use the canonical English entry name (Anxiety, not "ansia"), like the other canonical DR terms (CLAUDE.md §8). The copy itself is written in the brand's language.

### Rule 1 — Max 2-3 manifestations per copy passage

At any single moment in the copy where you're activating an emotional dimensionalization, use **at most 2-3 concrete cues** in the final output. More than that = narrative novel, not copy. Punchy, tight, sensory, focused.

### Rule 2 — Natural language in the copy

The cues in this file are descriptive, not finished sentences. The **final copy** must read in natural conversational language — the way a friend would actually describe a sensation to another friend at a bar. *"You feel your stomach close up"* works. A literary fragment like *"tightened stomach"* does not.

### Rule 3 — Mix dimensions

Pull from at least two of the three dimensions (**physical / mental / visceral**) for each moment. All-physical reads like body-language coaching. All-mental reads like inner monologue. All-visceral reads like a medical report. Spread them.

### Rule 4 — Never name the emotion directly

Show, don't tell. If you write *"she was anxious"* and then list anxious behaviors, you've already given the reader the answer. The reader should feel the emotion before they have a name for it.

### Rule 5 — Calibrate intensity

Each emotion in this file lists a continuum where useful (e.g. *unease → anxiety → paranoia → terror*). Pick the level that matches your scene. Mild discomfort and full terror require completely different cues.

### Rule 6 — Skip the clichés

If a cue in this file describes something that's been written a thousand times (*"a single tear rolled down her cheek"*), spin it into something fresher or skip it. This file is raw material, not a recipe.

### Rule 7 — Atomic descriptions, NOT ready-made copy lines ⚠️ MOST IMPORTANT

The cues below describe **how a manifestation works** — they are not finished sentences to be pasted into copy.

When you pull from this file, you MUST:
1. Read the atomic description of the manifestation
2. Understand the underlying dynamic it describes
3. **Regenerate** a fresh, specific cue in natural language, adapted to the character, the scene, the brand voice, and the moment in the copy you're writing

If you copy a cue verbatim, you've turned a database of anti-clichés into a database of clichés. Across enough pieces of copy, every brand starts to sound the same.

**Example of the right workflow:**

The file says:
> *"Compulsive checking of a source of expected news — clock, phone, door, inbox — without anything having actually changed."*

You're writing copy for a freelance copywriter waiting for a client's reply:
> *"Hai aperto WhatsApp per la quinta volta in dieci minuti. La spunta è ancora una sola."*

You're writing copy for a parent worried about a teenager who hasn't come home:
> *"La porta non si è ancora aperta. Sei rimasto in cucina a guardare quella porta come se fissarla potesse farla muovere."*

Same underlying cue, two completely different scenes. That's the point.

### Rule 8 — Anti-saturation across the piece

**Max 3 anchored rings per touchpoint, never two consecutive anchors from the same emotion family.** Beyond that threshold the reader stops feeling any of them — emotional saturation flattens every anchor into noise. This cap also governs branch (b) of "When to Consult This File" below (max 3 entries consulted per piece when the brief names no anchors).

## Before / After — Style Anchor

❌ **Wrong**: copying the cue from this file directly into copy.
> File says: *"Compulsive checking of a source of expected news — clock, phone, door, inbox..."*
> Copy: *"You keep checking your phone, even though you know nothing's changed."* (this is just the cue translated to "you" voice — generic, reusable across any copy → cliché in the making)

✅ **Right**: extracting the dynamic, regenerating it for the specific scene.
> File says: *"Compulsive checking of a source of expected news — clock, phone, door, inbox..."*
> Copy (freelance waiting on a client's wire transfer): *"Apri il conto. Lo chiudi. Lo apri di nuovo dopo trenta secondi. Lo stesso saldo che hai visto prima."*

The cue is the same. The execution is fresh every time.

## When to Consult This File

The lookup rule has two branches:

**(a) MANDATORY — when the brief's ring or touchpoint names Emotional anchors**: read those entries. The anchors are assigned in the funnel brief (§3.8 ring level, §4.2 / §4.3 touchpoint level); when they are there, consulting the named entries is not optional.

**(b) When the brief names no anchors** AND you are writing one of the 6 emotionally-led moments below: consult the relevant entries anyway (max 3 per piece — Rule 8) and **flag the missing anchors to the copywriter as a brief gap**.

The 6 emotionally-led moments:

1. **Problem dramatization** — when you're making the reader live the problem instead of describing it
2. **Identification hook / opening scene** — when the reader has to recognize themselves
3. **Future pacing** — when you're showing the "after" state of life with the product
4. **Objection / skepticism moment** — when the reader is mid-doubt and the copy needs to acknowledge it
5. **CTA emotional surge** — when the action you're asking for requires emotional charge (urgency, courage, determination)
6. **Emotional transition** — when the copy is moving the reader from one feeling to another and the shift needs to be felt, not stated

If neither branch applies at the current point of the copy, **this file is not the right tool**. Move on.

## Emotion Entries

---

### Anxiety

**Definition**: A low-burning fear about a future event or unresolved situation.
**Continuum**: unease → worry → anxiety → paranoia → terror

**Physical**:
- Compulsive checking of a source of expected news — clock, phone, door, inbox — without anything having actually changed
- Shoulders pulled up toward the ears, often without the person noticing
- Self-soothing oral behaviors — biting the cheek, the lip, the nails, the inside of the mouth
- Inability to settle into one position — constant shifting, weight changes, micro-adjustments

**Mental**:
- A worst-case scenario looping in the background of all other thought
- Reading silence or absence as confirmation of the feared outcome
- Pre-rehearsing conversations that may never happen
- A constant low-grade bracing for bad news

**Visceral**:
- Tightness or bracing in the stomach
- A knot or lump at the base of the throat that can't be swallowed away
- Shallow breathing held high in the chest
- A faint tension headache behind the eyes

**Suppressed signs**:
- Over-quick laughter at things that aren't funny
- Defaulting to "I'm fine" more often than usual

**Can escalate to**: Anguish, Terror (not in database — use the nearest entry: Fear, at the top of its continuum)

---

### Fear

**Definition**: A strong reaction to immediate danger or threat.
**Continuum**: unease → fear → terror

**Physical**:
- Eyes widening and scanning for the source of threat or for escape routes
- Involuntary backward movement of the body away from the perceived danger
- Hands curling inward, protecting the chest or face
- Voice dropping in volume, sometimes to near-whisper, sometimes muting entirely

**Mental**:
- Tunnel focus on the source of threat; surrounding sounds and voices fading
- Rapid mental mapping of exits, hiding places, or weapons
- Inability to plan beyond the next few seconds
- Distorted time perception — events feeling slower or jerkier than they are

**Visceral**:
- Pounding heart, audible to the person experiencing it
- Mouth going dry and sticky
- Cold spreading through the extremities — hands, feet, face
- A surge of adrenaline pressing the body toward flight or freeze

**Suppressed signs**:
- Unnatural stillness — the body becoming a held breath
- A controlled, flat voice that doesn't sound like the person's normal voice

**Can escalate to**: Terror (not in database — use the nearest entry: Fear itself, at the top of its continuum), Anguish

---

### Dread

**Definition**: A heavy, drawn-out fear of something that hasn't happened yet but is known to be coming.
**Continuum**: worry → dread → anguish → terror

**Physical**:
- Slowed movement, as if every step weighs more than the body
- Forward curl of the shoulders and collapse of the chest
- Excuses, delays, and avoidances aimed at postponing the feared moment
- Inability to make eye contact with the person or symbol that triggers the dread

**Mental**:
- A wish for time to skip ahead and be done
- Repeated mental rehearsal of the dreaded moment
- Pre-planning of how to survive the aftermath
- Bargaining with the self, with fate, with anyone perceived to have influence

**Visceral**:
- A heavy weight settling on the chest that doesn't lift
- A persistent ache at the back of the throat, near tears
- Cold, clumsy hands
- Sudden waking in the small hours, the dread remembered

**Suppressed signs**:
- Burying oneself in distraction — TV, books, scrolling, work — to not think
- Pretending the slowness and silence are just tiredness

**Can escalate to**: Anguish, Terror (not in database — use the nearest entry: Fear, at the top of its continuum)

---

### Worry

**Definition**: Persistent low-grade concern about something specific, usually for someone or something cared for.

**Physical**:
- Frequent glancing at clocks, doors, phones, or the place from which news would come
- Eyebrows drawn together without awareness
- Repeated touching of a single object — ring, pen, button — as a fidget
- Sudden silences mid-conversation as the worry pulls focus

**Mental**:
- Mental listing of everything that could go wrong, in order of likelihood
- Trying to remember what was missed, forgotten, or left unsaid
- Re-reading the last message or replaying the last conversation for hidden meaning
- Frequent "what if" framings of every present-tense thought

**Visceral**:
- Heaviness in the chest that interferes with deep breathing
- An unsettled stomach, vaguely on edge
- Jaw tension noticed only when it finally releases

**Can escalate to**: Anxiety, Dread

---

### Unease

**Definition**: A vague sense that something is wrong, without a clear referent.

**Physical**:
- Repeated checking over the shoulder, scanning the periphery
- Unconscious slowing of pace
- Hesitation at thresholds — doorways, corners, transitions between rooms
- The hand seeking something to hold — pocket, sleeve, strap

**Mental**:
- A sense of wrongness that resists being named
- Heightened alertness to small sounds, smells, and shifts
- A faint listening, as if for a signal not yet heard

**Visceral**:
- The hair on the back of the neck rising
- A lightness in the stomach, as if the floor moved an inch
- A small chill running along the spine

**Can escalate to**: Anxiety, Suspicion, Fear

---

### Nervousness

**Definition**: Jittery anticipation of a specific upcoming moment the person isn't sure they can handle.

**Physical**:
- Constant low-amplitude motion — foot tapping, finger drumming, knee bouncing
- Frequent throat-clearing without need
- Repetitive grooming gestures — smoothing the shirt, hair, the same spot of the desk
- Laughter at moments that don't warrant it

**Mental**:
- Mental rehearsal of what's about to be said, repeated and revised
- Vivid imagining of the moment going badly
- Loss of working memory for simple immediate tasks

**Visceral**:
- The "butterflies" sensation — a fluttering in the upper stomach
- Palm dampness that requires wiping
- Voice catching or wavering on the first words spoken

**Suppressed signs**:
- Overcompensating with too much eye contact
- Speaking faster than usual to push through the nervousness before it shows

**Can escalate to**: Anxiety, Fear

---

### Caution

**Definition**: Deliberate slowness and observation in a situation that hasn't yet proven safe.

**Physical**:
- Attention directed to hands and movement, not just faces
- Body oriented to keep exits in view; reluctance to sit until the space has been read
- Short, exploratory replies before any commitment to a position
- Possessions kept close — bag on lap, keys in hand

**Mental**:
- Active cataloging of details — names, positions, contradictions
- A baseline assumption that something could still be wrong
- Withholding commitment until enough evidence accumulates

**Visceral**:
- Sharpening of the senses — smells, sounds, peripheral movement
- A slightly elevated baseline heart rate without alarm

**Can escalate to**: Suspicion, Fear

---

### Agitation

**Definition**: Restless, jagged emotional energy without a clear target.

**Physical**:
- Inability to sit for more than seconds at a time
- Snapping at the nearest person over something disproportionately small
- Pacing without a destination, started without conscious decision
- Increased clumsiness — dropping, knocking over, fumbling

**Mental**:
- Inability to hold attention on what someone is saying for more than a few seconds
- A felt need to act without knowing on what
- Disproportionate magnification of small irritations

**Visceral**:
- A persistent tightness across the shoulders that won't release
- Elevated heart rate without provocation
- Flushed face even in a cool room

**Can escalate to**: Anxiety, Frustration, Anger

---

### Doubt

**Definition**: Hesitation about whether something is true or whether to commit.

**Physical**:
- A pause before any committing action — signing, clicking, nodding, speaking
- Asking the same question rephrased two or three different ways
- Re-reading the same sentence or clause multiple times
- Breaking eye contact with the person waiting for an answer

**Mental**:
- Weighing both sides without either feeling heavier
- Mental listing of reasons for and against in alternation
- A wish for one more piece of information that would settle it

**Visceral**:
- A held-breath quality in the chest
- A small pull in the stomach when imagining the act of committing

**Can escalate to**: Skepticism, Suspicion, Disbelief

---

### Uncertainty

**Definition**: Not knowing which way to go, with no strong pull toward either option.

**Physical**:
- Weight-shifting from foot to foot, or sitting position changing repeatedly
- Sentences begun and abandoned mid-thought
- Repeated shrugging in close succession
- Visual deferral to other people, looking for cues

**Mental**:
- A wish that someone would simply decide for the person
- Confusion between caution and avoidance
- Every option presenting a downside that feels disqualifying

**Visceral**:
- Foggy or sluggish thinking, like being underslept
- A mild, non-specific unsettled feeling in the stomach

**Can escalate to**: Doubt, Anxiety, Overwhelmed

---

### Skepticism

**Definition**: A learned reluctance to believe a claim, often shaped by past disappointment.

**Physical**:
- An involuntary lift of one eyebrow
- Arms crossing the chest as a barrier
- Leaning back, increasing physical distance from the speaker
- Tight half-smile accompanying a probing question

**Mental**:
- Active scanning for the catch, the omission, the too-good-to-be-true
- Pattern-matching the current claim against past failed promises
- Self-mistrust when wanting to believe — wanting it makes it suspect

**Visceral**:
- A slight tightening behind the jaw
- A small drop in the chest when something sounds too good

**Can escalate to**: Disbelief, Suspicion, Contempt

---

### Suspicion

**Definition**: An active sense that someone is concealing something or attempting deception.

**Physical**:
- Eyes tracking specific behaviors — hands, glance direction, micro-pauses
- Asking questions in reframed forms to test consistency of answers
- Withholding the social smile or warmth that would normally be reciprocated
- Body orientation pulling back or to the side rather than facing the suspected person fully

**Mental**:
- Hunting for the inconsistency between current and past statements
- Pre-deciding how to respond if the suspicion is confirmed
- Re-interpreting past interactions through the lens of possible deceit

**Visceral**:
- Heat building behind the ears
- A tightening in the chest that wasn't there at the start of the interaction

**Can escalate to**: Paranoia, Anger, Contempt

---

### Paranoia

**Definition**: A runaway belief about being targeted, watched, or set up — typically out of proportion to actual evidence.

**Physical**:
- Repeated and excessive verification of safety measures — locks, windows, accounts
- Editing or deleting communications before sending them
- Unnecessary route changes, schedule changes, identity changes
- Reduction of disclosure to people previously trusted

**Mental**:
- Connecting unrelated events into a coherent persecutory pattern
- Reading neutral behavior from others as targeted at the self
- Replaying recent conversations to identify what may have been "given away"

**Visceral**:
- Sudden heart racing in the absence of any actual trigger
- Light, broken sleep, easily disturbed by small sounds

**Can escalate to**: Fear, Terror (not in database — use the nearest entry: Fear, at the top of its continuum)

---

### Disbelief

**Definition**: The moment the mind refuses to accept what it has just heard or seen.

**Physical**:
- Mouth opening without speech following
- Asking the speaker to repeat themselves
- A short, sharp laugh disconnected from humor
- Head-shaking initiated before the response begins

**Mental**:
- Certainty that the words must have been misheard
- Frantic search for any alternative interpretation
- A sense that the established rules of reality have momentarily broken

**Visceral**:
- The ground feeling less solid underfoot
- A brief auditory dulling, as if submerged
- A wave of heat or cold passing through, direction undetermined

**Can escalate to**: Shock, Denial, Anger

---

### Confusion

**Definition**: An inability to make sense of what is happening.

**Physical**:
- Brow pinching together in the middle
- Head tilting to one side
- Interruptions with "wait" or "sorry" before realizing
- Visual checking with others in the room for shared understanding

**Mental**:
- Pieces of information that won't fit together as expected
- A sense of having missed a piece of context
- Mental rebuilding of the situation from the beginning

**Visceral**:
- A faint internal ringing or pressure in the ears
- Slowed cognitive tempo, as if thinking through water

**Can escalate to**: Frustration, Doubt, Anxiety

---

### Sadness

**Definition**: The quiet, heavy feeling of loss or wrongness that has settled in.

**Physical**:
- Loss of muscle tone in the face — slackness around the mouth, eyes, brow
- Eyes glossing over before any tear actually falls
- Loss of color and energy in the voice
- Choosing to sit in unusual places — floors, stairs, corners

**Mental**:
- Return to the same memory or thought, repeatedly
- A sense of distance from the parts of life that are still good
- Simultaneous desire to be alone and desire not to be alone

**Visceral**:
- A heaviness in the chest that resists naming
- A real ache in the throat, not connected to illness
- A burning in the eyes that doesn't always produce tears

**Suppressed signs**:
- An over-quick, over-bright smile when caught looking sad
- Skilled subject-changing whenever the subject approaches

**Can escalate to**: Depression, Despair

---

### Depression

**Definition**: Prolonged sadness that has become baseline, with motivation drained.

**Physical**:
- Extended time in bed beyond normal sleep
- Accumulation of undone domestic tasks the person is aware of and does not address
- Reduction or cessation of personal grooming routines
- Read but unanswered messages from people who matter

**Mental**:
- Nothing presenting as worth doing
- Inability to recall the last time something genuinely felt good
- Loss of interest in connecting with people previously close

**Visceral**:
- Tiredness that sleep does not relieve
- Loss of taste pleasure, or monotonous overeating of one thing
- A bodily heaviness disproportionate to actual weight

**Suppressed signs**:
- The "I'm fine" performance maintained over weeks
- Over-commitment to plans, followed by late cancellations

**Can escalate to**: Despair

---

### Despair

**Definition**: The floor of sadness, where belief in possible improvement collapses.

**Physical**:
- Cessation of crying even when crying would be expected
- Giving away or discarding things previously valued
- A monotone voice, even on subjects that would normally animate
- Delayed or absent response to one's own name

**Mental**:
- Stopping all forward planning, even short-term
- Imagining one's absence and finding the world adequate without it
- Cessation of arguments and protests, even justified ones

**Visceral**:
- A hollowing of the chest, distinct from heaviness
- Loss of awareness of one's own physical comfort or discomfort
- Sleep that fails to restore

**Can escalate to**: This is already the floor of this family.

---

### Anguish

**Definition**: Acute mental and emotional pain that the person cannot escape.

**Physical**:
- Hands going to the head and remaining there
- Sounds that aren't words — moans, groans, the breath drawn raggedly
- A single hard impact — fist to wall, palm to surface — without sustained violence
- An inability to remain in one room; circulating through the house and returning

**Mental**:
- Compulsive replay of the moment the wound was inflicted
- A wish to reverse time by ten minutes, days, years
- Inability to land on any thought that is not the wound

**Visceral**:
- Whole-body ache without a specific localized pain
- Breath pulled in short, snatched gulps
- Real chest pain triggered by the memory

**Can escalate to**: This is already one of the most extreme states in this family.

---

### Melancholy

**Definition**: A soft, settled sadness — quieter than depression, sometimes near-comfortable.

**Physical**:
- Sustained gazing at a view or scene beyond its merit
- Replaying of music or media tied to a past mood
- Aimless walks without destination
- A small, tired smile rather than absence of expression

**Mental**:
- Remembrance of a version of life that no longer exists
- A sense of nearness to something that can't be touched
- Slowed thought without distress about the slowness

**Visceral**:
- A soft heaviness in the chest that is not unwelcome
- Damp eyes without active crying

**Can escalate to**: Sadness, Depression

---

### Nostalgia

**Definition**: Sweet sadness for a past that cannot be returned to.

**Physical**:
- Handling of objects connected to the period being remembered
- Scrolling through old photos, old letters, old correspondence
- Reaching out to people long out of contact
- Smiling at things only the person remembers

**Mental**:
- Polishing the memory beyond what it was
- Counterfactual exploration — what if things had stayed
- Wondering whether the other people in the memory remember it the same way

**Visceral**:
- A warm pull in the chest, both pleasant and aching
- Damp eyes appearing unexpectedly

**Can escalate to**: Melancholy, Sadness

---

### Loneliness

**Definition**: Awareness of disconnection from the people one would want close.

**Physical**:
- Scrolling through contacts without finding anyone to actually reach
- Use of media — TV, radio, podcasts — as ambient company
- Eating standing up, eating quickly, eating without ceremony
- Early bedtime to shorten the day

**Mental**:
- A sense that everyone else has someone except the self
- Doubt about whether anyone would notice a withdrawal
- Missing people who treated the person badly, because the alternative is no one

**Visceral**:
- A hollow feeling in the chest that doesn't fill
- A felt coldness independent of room temperature

**Can escalate to**: Sadness, Depression

---

### Hurt

**Definition**: Emotional pain following a wound from someone trusted.

**Physical**:
- Going quiet at the point where pushback would normally occur
- Leaving the room without explanation
- Avoiding eye contact with the person responsible
- Short, two-word answers to anything they ask

**Mental**:
- Re-running their words in search of a less wounding interpretation
- Deciding whether to disclose the impact or hide it
- A sense that the warning signs should have been spotted

**Visceral**:
- A real, localized ache in the chest
- Sudden, unexpected heat behind the eyes

**Suppressed signs**:
- "It's fine" delivered without eye contact
- Laughing it off in the moment, going silent ten minutes later

**Can escalate to**: Sadness, Anger, Resentment

---

### Pity

**Definition**: Sorrow for another's suffering, mixed with a sense of distance from it.

**Physical**:
- Softened vocal tone reserved for the person
- A light touch on the arm or hand
- Sustained eye contact longer than usual
- Absence of normal joking or lightness around them

**Mental**:
- Guilty relief that it is not happening to the self
- Search for something offerable that won't ring hollow
- Awareness of being unable to actually fix the situation

**Visceral**:
- A small heaviness in the chest, distinct from grief
- Slight tightening behind the eyes

**Can escalate to**: Compassion, Sadness

---

### Regret

**Definition**: Wishing one had chosen differently.

**Physical**:
- Spontaneous head-shaking, often when alone
- Hand covering the mouth at the moment of remembrance
- Sentences abandoned and restarted
- Restless transition between rooms or activities

**Mental**:
- Replaying the moment of the decision in search of an alternative version
- Listing all who were let down by the choice
- Uncertainty about whether anyone else remembers it as clearly

**Visceral**:
- A drop in the chest at each return of the memory
- A turn of the stomach

**Can escalate to**: Remorse, Guilt, Despair

---

### Shame

**Definition**: The sense that something about the self, not just the act, is wrong.

**Physical**:
- Loss of eye contact across the board, not just with the person involved
- Voice dropped below normal speaking volume
- Bodily contraction — arms in, shoulders down, head lowered
- Premature exit from situations once a way out can be found

**Mental**:
- Certainty that others are discussing the failure in absence
- Loop replay of the moment of exposure
- Strong desire to undo not the act, but the being-seen

**Visceral**:
- Heat across the face and ears beyond conscious control
- A squeezed feeling in the chest
- Mild nausea

**Suppressed signs**:
- Overcorrection through performed brightness and volume
- Aggressive subject-changing twice in succession

**Can escalate to**: Humiliation, Self-loathing (not in database — use the nearest entry: Shame itself, at chronic intensity)

---

### Guilt

**Definition**: The heavy awareness of having caused harm by choice.

**Physical**:
- Avoidance of eye contact specifically with the person wronged
- Disproportionate apologies for small things, as displacement
- Volunteering for tasks normally avoided
- Unnecessary physical contact — pats, brushes — as silent restitution

**Mental**:
- Compulsive replay of the act
- Mental rehearsal of the other person's experience of the harm
- Imagining confession and bracing for the consequence

**Visceral**:
- A turn of the stomach at the sight of the person
- Throat tightening when their name comes up

**Suppressed signs**:
- Unusual generosity without traceable reason
- Inability to enjoy good fortune without thought of the wronged party

**Can escalate to**: Remorse, Shame

---

### Remorse

**Definition**: Deep regret for harm caused, paired with the desire to repair.

**Physical**:
- Drafting an apology in writing before delivering it aloud
- Sleeplessness before the conversation of accountability
- Bringing a small offering when going to face the wronged person
- Voice breaking on the first attempt to speak

**Mental**:
- Awareness of what would be given to undo it, and that nothing can
- Multiple rehearsed openings, none feeling adequate
- A shift in self-definition based on the act and its acknowledgment

**Visceral**:
- A weight in the chest that resists shifting
- A persistent ache in the throat when speaking on the subject

**Can escalate to**: Anguish, Despair

---

### Humiliation

**Definition**: Shame caused by public exposure or diminishment.

**Physical**:
- Fixed downward gaze
- Trembling in hands during simple tasks immediately afterward
- Premature retreat from the social space to somewhere private
- Distrust of one's own voice for some time after

**Mental**:
- Imagining the event being replayed by others, later, in absence
- Calculating whether the social space can be re-entered
- A desire to disappear that is alarming in its intensity

**Visceral**:
- Sustained facial heat lasting minutes after the moment
- Physical sickness — a nausea wave, sometimes more
- Auditory dulling during and just after the event

**Can escalate to**: Shame, Rage, Despair

---

### Anger

**Definition**: A sharp emotional response to perceived violation, unfairness, or threat.

**Physical**:
- Clenched jaw, often noticed only when it releases
- Standing or rising more quickly than the moment required
- Voice changes — louder, sharper, or unusually quiet and low
- Pointing or sharp gesturing not characteristic of the person's normal range

**Mental**:
- Pre-rehearsing what one wants to say
- Cataloging prior offenses by the same source
- Reduced concern for consequences of the response

**Visceral**:
- Heat moving up through the chest and into the face
- A hammering heart rate
- Hot, almost itchy hands

**Suppressed signs**:
- Going unusually quiet and still
- Wide smile delivered with cutting words

**Can escalate to**: Rage, Hatred, Contempt

---

### Rage

**Definition**: Anger that has lost containment, where action precedes thought.

**Physical**:
- Destruction of an object within reach
- Voice rendered unrecognizable in pitch and volume
- Visible tremor in the hands once the peak passes
- Inability to recall the specific words said

**Mental**:
- Complete narrowing to a single target
- Total loss of consequence-weighing
- A felt sense of full moral justification, regardless of context

**Visceral**:
- Heart rate at near-sprint levels
- Adrenaline-driven hand tingling
- Sweating through the clothing

**Can escalate to**: This is already the extreme.

---

### Frustration

**Definition**: Anger mixed with helplessness — when effort keeps not yielding result.

**Physical**:
- Hands thrown up, palms out
- A single hard impact — fist on table — without sustained violence
- Walking away from the task to a different room
- Restarting the task with worse posture and shorter patience

**Mental**:
- Awareness that multiple attempts have failed and the cause may not be findable
- Difficulty distinguishing between problem-with-the-thing and problem-with-the-self
- Simultaneous urge to quit and inability to quit

**Visceral**:
- Tight heat behind the eyes
- Jaw soreness from sustained clenching
- Shoulders rising toward the ears

**Can escalate to**: Anger, Resignation

---

### Resentment

**Definition**: Slow-burning anger toward someone for an unresolved old hurt.

**Physical**:
- Withdrawal of normal favors and kindnesses
- Cessation of laughter at the person's jokes
- Avoidance of rooms they enter
- Carefully neutral tone — never warm, never overtly cold

**Mental**:
- Internal revisiting of the original offense more often than would be admitted
- A sour internal reaction to the person's successes
- Suspicion that the offender doesn't remember the offense at all

**Visceral**:
- Tightness across the shoulders when they're present
- A bad taste in the mouth, almost literally

**Suppressed signs**:
- Strained politeness — more correct than warm
- Public praise of the person, delivered with a slight catch

**Can escalate to**: Bitterness (not in database — use the nearest entry: Resentment itself, hardened over time), Hatred, Contempt

---

### Irritation

**Definition**: Low-grade annoyance — the stage just before anger.

**Physical**:
- Audible sighing
- Hardware handled with more force than required — laptop closed too hard, doors not quite slammed
- Shorter sentences than normal in response
- Absence of normal greeting reciprocation

**Mental**:
- Disproportionate magnification of small intrusions
- Wish for solitude that is harder to mask than usual
- Calculation of the shortest exit from the current interaction

**Visceral**:
- Tight forehead
- Shoulders sitting higher than at rest

**Can escalate to**: Frustration, Anger

---

### Annoyance

**Definition**: A small spike of displeasure at something mildly disruptive.

**Physical**:
- Eye-roll, often performed when unobserved
- Hard exhalation through the nose
- Slight, dismissive head-shake
- Muttered comment under the breath

**Mental**:
- Mental pre-drafting of the complaint to be told later, with a better punchline
- A sense that the person should know better

**Visceral**:
- Slight jaw tension

**Can escalate to**: Irritation, Frustration

---

### Contempt

**Definition**: The cold conviction that someone or something is beneath one's regard.

**Physical**:
- Brief eye contact followed by deliberate looking past or through
- Asymmetrical mouth lift — one corner only
- Unanswered questions, silence used as response
- Leaving while the other person is still speaking

**Mental**:
- A pre-decision that the other party isn't worth the energy of polite engagement
- Surprise that anyone in the room is taking the person seriously
- Absence of curiosity about anything the person might know

**Visceral**:
- A coolness across the chest, the opposite of warmth

**Can escalate to**: Scorn, Hatred

---

### Scorn

**Definition**: Open, demonstrated contempt.

**Physical**:
- A single short, humorless laugh
- Head-shake combined with shoulder turning away
- Audible commentary about the person to others, without lowering volume
- Direct gaze held while delivering a cutting remark

**Mental**:
- Intent to be heard and seen in the disdain
- No interest in softening the delivery
- A felt sense that softening would dilute the point

**Visceral**:
- A cool, controlled energy in the chest

**Can escalate to**: Hatred

---

### Hatred

**Definition**: Deep-rooted, sustained contempt and ill will toward a person or thing.

**Physical**:
- Active avoidance of physical proximity, even at social cost
- Refusal to use the person's name
- Visible satisfaction at their misfortune
- Inability to maintain civility for more than the briefest required interval

**Mental**:
- Compulsive cataloging of every wrong attributed to the target
- Active imagining of futures in which the target fails
- Loss of memory for when forgiveness was last considered possible

**Visceral**:
- Heat rising in the chest at the mention of the name
- A metallic or sour taste in the mouth

**Can escalate to**: This is already the extreme.

---

### Envy

**Definition**: The painful awareness that someone has what one wants and lacks.

**Physical**:
- Tightened grip when scrolling past their content
- Flat-toned congratulations, the flatness audible
- Subject-changing when their name comes up
- Avoidance of events celebrating them

**Mental**:
- Compulsive measuring of the gap between them and the self
- Internal generation of reasons they don't deserve it
- Vivid imagining of life from inside their position

**Visceral**:
- A bitter twist in the stomach at their good news
- Face warmth the person hopes is invisible

**Suppressed signs**:
- Excessive public praise of the target
- Probing questions hoping to surface a flaw

**Can escalate to**: Resentment, Jealousy, Hatred

---

### Disgust

**Definition**: Strong revulsion at something perceived as offensive, unclean, or wrong.

**Physical**:
- Wrinkling of the nose
- Curl of the upper lip
- A step backward, away from the source
- Wiping of the hand even when nothing touched it

**Mental**:
- An immediate wish to be elsewhere
- Memory-stamping for later retelling and complaint
- Refusal of belief that what is being witnessed is real

**Visceral**:
- A wave of nausea, even when mild
- Saliva flooding in the way that precedes gagging

**Can escalate to**: Contempt, Hatred (when disgust attaches to a person)

---

### Denial

**Definition**: Refusal to accept a truth too painful or threatening to integrate.

**Physical**:
- Out-of-place laughter, sharp and short
- Head-shaking before the speaker has finished
- Aggressive subject-change within seconds
- A sudden need to clean, organize, or busy the hands

**Mental**:
- Rejection of every version of the story being told
- Generation of reasons the report cannot be accurate
- Imagining the speaker's eventual apology for being wrong

**Visceral**:
- A strange numbness in the hands
- Pressure in the chest that has not yet resolved into grief

**Can escalate to**: Sadness, Anger, Despair (as the truth lands)

---

### Defensiveness

**Definition**: Automatic protective reaction to perceived criticism or threat.

**Physical**:
- Arms crossing unconsciously
- Shoulders rising, chest pulling backward
- Interruption before the other person has finished
- Locked jaw

**Mental**:
- Pre-explaining the self before the accusation has fully landed
- Cataloging of the other person's faults as a counterweight
- A felt need to win the exchange rather than understand it

**Visceral**:
- Spike in heart rate
- Heat rising in the chest

**Suppressed signs**:
- Unnaturally controlled calm, with very precise word choice

**Can escalate to**: Anger, Resentment

---

### Indifference

**Definition**: The absence of caring where caring would be expected.

**Physical**:
- A shrug in response to news that should land
- Continued scrolling or unrelated activity during the disclosure
- Flat facial expression
- Absence of follow-up questions

**Mental**:
- Inability to locate a reason to care
- Already mentally moved on, before the conversation has ended
- Quiet wondering why the matter is significant to anyone else

**Visceral**:
- Nothing. The absence of internal reaction is the signal.

**Can escalate to**: This is often itself the destination — a defensive flatness once feeling has shut down.

---

### Overwhelmed

**Definition**: The state of facing more demands than capacity, all feeling urgent simultaneously.

**Physical**:
- Sitting still in front of a list, screen, or room without acting
- Standing up and sitting down without completing an intermediate task
- Multiple tasks begun, none finished
- Snapping at someone over a disproportionately small trigger

**Mental**:
- Every task presenting as urgent, so none can be selected
- A growing rather than shrinking task list
- A sense of being behind from the moment of waking

**Visceral**:
- Tightness across the forehead
- Elevated heart rate without provocation
- Physical heaviness on the shoulders

**Suppressed signs**:
- Insistence on being "just busy"
- Quiet withdrawal and last-minute cancellation of commitments

**Can escalate to**: Anxiety, Despair, Resignation

---

### Conflicted

**Definition**: Being pulled strongly in two directions at once.

**Physical**:
- Pacing
- Drafts started and deleted
- Same person consulted for advice multiple times in short succession
- Disrupted sleep, including unusual sleep position

**Mental**:
- Case-building for one side, then the other, on alternation
- Both options simultaneously feeling correct and wrong
- A wish for external decision-making

**Visceral**:
- A tightness in the chest that doesn't release with either prospective choice

**Can escalate to**: Overwhelmed, Anxiety

---

### Resignation

**Definition**: Accepting an undesired outcome because continued struggle has lost its value.

**Physical**:
- Shoulders dropping and staying down
- Mid-sentence cessation of argument followed by a nod
- Signing or agreeing without further reading
- A long, slow exhale

**Mental**:
- Cessation of scenario-running
- Realization that grief had begun earlier, only unacknowledged
- Stopping the search for new alternatives

**Visceral**:
- A heaviness in the chest that is almost calming
- Full release of muscle tension that had been holding for days

**Can escalate to**: This is often itself the destination — a release valve from prolonged struggle.

---

### Reluctance

**Definition**: Hesitation to do something because of unwillingness, even when obligation is clear.

**Physical**:
- Being the last to volunteer
- Leaving the room when the topic arises
- Slowed pace in the approach to the required action
- Multiple "maybe" answers before eventual yes

**Mental**:
- Hope that someone else will assume the responsibility
- Listing of reasons the obligation shouldn't apply
- Internal negotiation with the self

**Visceral**:
- A backward pull in the chest, as if something is resisting forward motion

**Can escalate to**: Resentment, Anger

---

### Disappointment

**Definition**: The gap between expectation and actual, when actual is worse.

**Physical**:
- A flat "ah, okay" with the voice not quite normal
- Visible drop in shoulder position
- Eye contact broken with the messenger
- Quick subject-changing

**Mental**:
- Realization of how much expectation had been built
- Difficulty deciding whether disappointment is in them or in the self
- Adjustment of plans previously not consciously made

**Visceral**:
- A small drop in the chest, like a missed step in the dark
- Heaviness behind the eyes that approaches tears without arriving

**Can escalate to**: Sadness, Resignation

---

### Insecurity

**Definition**: Persistent doubt about being adequate for the room, the role, or the relationship.

**Physical**:
- Repeated checking of one's reflection or appearance
- Apologies for things that don't require apology
- Volume dropping in the presence of a perceived authority
- Bodily contraction — arms in, shoulders down — without awareness

**Mental**:
- Worry about being discovered as not belonging
- Comparative cataloging of everyone else's apparent confidence
- Hypervigilance about one's own tone, posture, and word choice

**Visceral**:
- Stomach tightening when attention lingers on the person
- Facial warmth when asked to speak

**Suppressed signs**:
- Overcompensation through eager agreement, premature laughter

**Can escalate to**: Anxiety, Shame, Failure

---

### Failure

**Definition**: The felt sense of having fallen short — not just at a task, but as a person.

**Physical**:
- Inability to face the people who were counting on the outcome
- Deletion of work, posts, drafts before exposure
- Withdrawal to private space, fixed gaze on nothing
- Unanswered phone calls

**Mental**:
- Listing of everyone disappointed
- Comparison with the self who had promised to succeed
- Calculation of whether retry is possible or warranted

**Visceral**:
- Whole-body heaviness
- Burning at the back of the eyes

**Can escalate to**: Despair, Depression

---

### Jealousy

**Definition**: Fear of losing something — typically someone — to a perceived rival.

**Physical**:
- Surveillance of the partner's interactions with the perceived rival
- Casual but repeated mention of the rival
- Increased physical proximity to the partner when the rival is present
- Compulsion to check the partner's communications

**Mental**:
- Replay of every observed interaction between the two
- Worst-case interpretation of every silence
- Decision-making about disclosure vs. waiting

**Visceral**:
- Hot tightness in the chest that resists release
- Real physical sickness at seeing them together

**Suppressed signs**:
- Performed cheerfulness in front of both
- Mental silence for hours afterward

**Can escalate to**: Anger, Resentment, Despair

---

### Curiosity

**Definition**: The pull toward something one wants to understand better.

**Physical**:
- Forward lean of the upper body
- Questions asked before others can ask first
- Loss of attention to phone, watch, or competing stimuli
- Sustained eye tracking of the speaker

**Mental**:
- Wish for the speaker to continue
- Pre-formation of follow-up questions
- Time speeding past unnoticed

**Visceral**:
- A small lift or brightness in the chest
- Slightly quickened breathing

**Can escalate to**: Excitement, Eagerness

---

### Amazement

**Definition**: Being struck by something previously thought impossible.

**Physical**:
- Wide eyes that remain wide
- Slight mouth opening
- Cessation of motion
- Lost track of what was about to be said

**Mental**:
- Effort to reconcile what was witnessed with prior beliefs about the possible
- Immediate desire to share the experience
- Sense of having witnessed something rare

**Visceral**:
- A warm wave through the chest
- Faint skin tingling

**Can escalate to**: Awe (not in database — use the nearest entry: Amazement itself, at maximum intensity), Excitement

---

### Surprise

**Definition**: A brief, instant reaction to the unexpected.

**Physical**:
- Eyebrows shooting up
- A small startle even when nothing dangerous
- A sharp, short laugh
- Request for repetition of what was just said

**Mental**:
- Brief lag while the brain catches up
- Confirmation-seeking — making sure it was heard correctly

**Visceral**:
- A brief heart-jump
- Flutter through the chest

**Can escalate to**: Shock, Disbelief, Excitement (depending on the nature of the surprise)

---

### Shock

**Definition**: Surprise that overloads the system — typically when the news is very bad or very good and the body can't process fast enough.

**Physical**:
- Freezing in place
- Hand to mouth without conscious decision
- Color draining from or flooding the face
- Multiple requests for repetition of the statement

**Mental**:
- Quieting of normal thought stream
- Inability to recall what was being done seconds earlier
- Hearing one's own voice make sounds before the words are chosen

**Visceral**:
- Narrowing of vision, dulling of peripheral awareness
- A wave of cold or heat passing through the chest
- A momentary forgetting of breath

**Can escalate to**: Disbelief, Despair, Anguish (if bad) or Elation (if good)

---

### Determination

**Definition**: Settled commitment to push through what stands between the self and a chosen goal.

**Physical**:
- A set jaw
- Forward-sitting posture, both feet planted
- Shorter, sharper sentences
- Unbroken eye contact in the face of attempts to dissuade

**Mental**:
- Cessation of entertaining doubts
- Cessation of route-comparing — the route has been picked
- Cessation of asking permission

**Visceral**:
- A low, steady warmth in the chest
- Slower, deeper breathing

**Can escalate to**: Eagerness, Single-mindedness (not in database — use the nearest entry: Determination itself, at maximum intensity)

---

### Desire

**Definition**: The pull toward something — a person, an object, a state — not yet possessed.

**Physical**:
- Lingering attention on the object beyond its merit
- Repeated mention of it in conversation
- Vivid imagining of possessing it
- Detour or rerouting to be near it

**Mental**:
- Case-building for why the self deserves it
- Listing of effort or merit accumulated
- Detailed imagining of the moment of acquisition

**Visceral**:
- A warm ache in the chest, sometimes lower
- Breath shallowing at the thought of it

**Can escalate to**: Obsession (not in database — use the nearest entry: Desire itself, at maximum intensity), Eagerness

---

### Excitement

**Definition**: High-energy positive anticipation, where the body wants to move before the moment arrives.

**Physical**:
- Inability to remain still
- Faster speech, with self-interruption
- Laughter triggered by lower-threshold stimulus than usual
- Increased physical contact — hugs, touches — beyond habit

**Mental**:
- Difficulty attending to anything else
- Premature planning of details
- Earlier waking with immediate recall of the cause

**Visceral**:
- Heart rate slightly elevated at rest
- Buzz under the skin, especially in the chest

**Suppressed signs**:
- Performed calm that registers as artificially flat
- Double-asking about another person's unrelated topic

**Can escalate to**: Eagerness, Elation

---

### Anticipation

**Definition**: Forward-leaning waiting for something believed to be coming.

**Physical**:
- Repeated checking of time, door, inbox
- Pre-arrangement — clothing, route, opening line
- Private smiling

**Mental**:
- Pre-playing of how the moment will unfold
- Rehearsal of one's best version of the self
- Reduced concentration on the present task

**Visceral**:
- A warm tightness in the chest, like a held breath that doesn't strain

**Can escalate to**: Excitement, Eagerness, Anxiety (if expectation tilts negative)

---

### Impatience

**Definition**: The friction of waiting for something wanted now.

**Physical**:
- Repeated time-checking at short intervals
- Tapping — foot, fingers, surfaces
- Starting the next task before completing the current one
- Faster speech with shorter sentences

**Mental**:
- Calculation of remaining wait
- Disproportionate irritation at minor delays
- Imagining of what would already be done if the wait were over

**Visceral**:
- Tight buzz across the shoulders
- Jaw clenching and releasing in pulses

**Can escalate to**: Frustration, Anger

---

### Eagerness

**Definition**: Open, leaning-forward willingness to do or have something.

**Physical**:
- Being first to raise the hand or volunteer
- Forward lean into the table or conversation
- Agreement before the proposal has been fully stated
- Early arrival

**Mental**:
- No need for persuasion
- Pre-imagining of one's own participation
- Wish for the explaining to end and the doing to begin

**Visceral**:
- A small lift in the chest
- Pre-exertion deepening of breath

**Can escalate to**: Excitement, Determination

---

### Happiness

**Definition**: A positive emotional state ranging from contentment to delight.

**Physical**:
- Easy, frequent smiling, including when no one is watching
- Humming, whistling, singing unconsciously
- Lighter, easier movement
- Pausing for small details normally walked past

**Mental**:
- Default-positive interpretation of others' behavior
- Generosity exceeding usual caution
- Self-aware noticing that this is good

**Visceral**:
- Sustained warmth in the chest
- Deep, easy breathing

**Can escalate to**: Elation, Satisfaction

---

### Satisfaction

**Definition**: The settled fullness after getting or doing what one wanted.

**Physical**:
- Sitting back, releasing forward posture
- Long, slow exhale
- Taking time over the final portion of food, drink, task
- No reach for the phone

**Mental**:
- Pause on planning the next thing
- Allowing the felt experience of the completion
- No need for external validation

**Visceral**:
- A warm settling through the chest
- Complete shoulder drop

**Can escalate to**: This is often itself the destination — the resting state after effort.

---

### Relief

**Definition**: The release that comes when a feared outcome doesn't happen.

**Physical**:
- Long exhale of breath previously held
- Hand to chest or to forehead
- A short, near-embarrassed laugh
- Sitting down even when standing was intended

**Mental**:
- Slight delay in believing it
- Replay of the moment of finding out
- Realization of how much weight had been carried

**Visceral**:
- Warm wave through the entire body
- Shoulder drop of visible distance
- Light, almost dizzying sensation in the chest

**Can escalate to**: Elation, Happiness, Gratitude

---

### Peacefulness

**Definition**: A calm, untroubled state — nothing pulling at the self.

**Physical**:
- Shoulders at full natural position
- Loss of awareness of phone over extended interval
- More listening than speaking
- Naturally lengthened breath

**Mental**:
- No felt need to be elsewhere
- Thoughts arriving and leaving without being grasped
- Cessation of problem-solving

**Visceral**:
- Slow, even chest rhythm
- Sustained warmth without source

**Can escalate to**: This is often itself the destination — frequently the unstated goal.

---

### Amusement

**Definition**: Light enjoyment of something funny, clever, or absurd.

**Physical**:
- Laughter, sometimes against the wish to withhold it
- Head-shake combined with smile
- Looking away to compose, then back, still smiling
- Sharing the moment with someone else later

**Mental**:
- Enjoyment of the absurdity itself
- Pre-formation of the story for retelling
- Forgiveness of the responsible party, mostly

**Visceral**:
- Warmth across the face
- A small bounce in the chest

**Can escalate to**: Happiness, Excitement

---

### Gratitude

**Definition**: Warm awareness of having been given something unearned.

**Physical**:
- Thanks delivered with eye contact and meaning
- Lingering in the moment of leaving
- Bringing something the next time

**Mental**:
- Awareness of how much the other person did
- Spillover — thinking of others who should be thanked
- Brief pause to feel grateful for normally-overlooked things

**Visceral**:
- Soft warmth in the chest
- Damp eyes that may or may not produce tears

**Can escalate to**: Love, Adoration

---

### Elation

**Definition**: Intense happiness, often surprising in its magnitude.

**Physical**:
- Inability to stop smiling, even when situationally inappropriate
- Hugging people not normally hugged
- Physical movement — jumping, fist-pumping, vocal exclamation — keyed to personality
- Immediate impulse to call someone specific

**Mental**:
- Disbelief that the outcome is real
- Replay of the announcing moment
- Brief sense that anything is now possible

**Visceral**:
- Chest warmth bordering on ache
- Racing heart in the positive register
- Momentary sense of lightness exceeding the body

**Can escalate to**: This is already the extreme.

---

### Optimism

**Definition**: Steady belief that things will work out, in advance of proof.

**Physical**:
- Smiling at strangers
- Early volunteering
- Longer-range planning
- Reduced consumption of bad news

**Mental**:
- Default assumption of good intent
- Favorable imagining of every uncertain outcome
- Cessation of worst-case scenario running

**Visceral**:
- Low, quiet warmth, sustained
- Deep, easy breathing

**Can escalate to**: Excitement, Eagerness

---

### Hope

**Definition**: The fragile, forward-reaching belief that a better outcome is still possible — held without proof, often against recent evidence.
**Continuum**: resignation → wishfulness → hope → optimism → anticipation

**Physical**:
- Re-opening something already given up on — the saved job posting, the old chat thread, the drawer that was closed for good
- One small preparation made for the desired outcome, downplayed out loud ("just in case")
- Leaning or stepping toward the source of possible good news
- A guarded half-smile the person tries to suppress

**Mental**:
- Allowing the imagining of the better outcome only in short bursts, retreating quickly to protect against disappointment
- Re-weighing the same evidence, with new weight quietly given to the "for" column
- Private bargaining — "if this works, I'll..."
- Permission, newly granted, to sketch a plan that depends on the outcome

**Visceral**:
- A light lift in the chest, fragile and easy to lose
- Breath catching slightly when the possibility is named aloud
- A flutter low in the stomach, distinct from dread by its warmth

**Suppressed signs**:
- Dismissing the possibility out loud while continuing to check on it
- Refusing to say the wish aloud, as if naming it could break it

**Can escalate to**: Optimism, Anticipation, Excitement

---

### Love

**Definition**: Deep, settled care for another, paired with desire to protect, support, or stay close.

**Physical**:
- Watching them when they don't know they're being watched
- Memory for their small things — schedule, coffee, recent friend trouble
- Adjustment of own plans without resentment
- More physical contact — hand on back, brush of hair

**Mental**:
- Imagining life without them and finding it sickening
- Forgiveness extended beyond what would be extended to anyone else
- Wanting to give them things they haven't asked for

**Visceral**:
- A warm pull in the chest at their entrance to the room
- Slow, deep peace when they sleep beside

**Can escalate to**: Adoration

---

### Adoration

**Definition**: Love mixed with awe — where the other person feels almost too valuable to be real.

**Physical**:
- An uncharacteristic softness in the gaze
- Laughter at jokes that don't fully merit it
- Learning their interests just to be closer
- Photographing them when they aren't aware

**Mental**:
- Felt luck bordering on disquiet
- Inability to fully believe in their presence
- Wish for others to perceive them the same way

**Visceral**:
- Near-constant warm ache in the chest

**Can escalate to**: This is already the extreme of this family.

---

### Compassion

**Definition**: Sorrow for another's suffering, paired with the desire to help.

**Physical**:
- Softened vocal tone
- Sitting beside the suffering person rather than standing
- A hand on the arm, kept there
- Staying longer than initially intended

**Mental**:
- Detailed imagining of what the other is enduring
- Considering what would actually help vs. what is expected
- Willingness to sit with the suffering rather than rush to fix

**Visceral**:
- Warm heaviness in the chest, sharing the weight
- Hot eyes, occasionally tearing

**Can escalate to**: Love, Sadness

---

### Pride

**Definition**: A positive sense of accomplishment, in self or in someone close.

**Physical**:
- More upright posture
- Harder-to-hide smile
- Retelling of the story to one more person
- Self-rewarding behavior — small purchase, special meal

**Mental**:
- Allowing the felt experience of the work that produced the outcome
- Thinking of those who should know, who would be proud
- Pre-imagining the next attempt with elevated confidence

**Visceral**:
- A broad, warm sensation across the chest
- Deep, even breathing

**Can escalate to**: Smugness (if it tips into superiority), Elation

---

### Smugness

**Definition**: Pride that has tipped into looking down on those who didn't achieve the same.

**Physical**:
- Slight one-sided mouth lift
- Small private nod at one's own thought
- Under-distribution of credit when credit is shared
- Inserting the achievement into conversations where it doesn't belong

**Mental**:
- Comparison with those who didn't make it
- Outsize pleasure in being correct
- Reduced openness to learning from anyone in the room

**Visceral**:
- Chest warmth of a cooler register than ordinary pride

**Can escalate to**: Contempt

### Confidence

**Definition**: Settled trust in one's own ability to handle what is in front of one.

**Physical**:
- Entering a room without scanning it first
- Speaking first when timing makes sense
- Eye contact made and held without effort
- Taking space without calculation

**Mental**:
- Reduced second-guessing of the first instinct
- Absence of comparative running against others in the room
- Ready knowledge of what one would say if things went wrong

**Visceral**:
- Steady, calm chest rhythm
- Deep breathing, relaxed jaw

**Can escalate to**: Smugness (if it loses humility), Pride

## Final Note

This file is a living document. New emotions, refined cues, and better atomic descriptions get added over time. The point is not to be exhaustive — it's to give the system enough raw material to dimensionalize a feeling at the exact moment the copy needs it.

Used correctly, this file makes copy land like a real human voice talking to another real human. Used incorrectly — by copying cues verbatim instead of regenerating them — it turns the copy into a database of body-language clichés.

The difference is always **Rule 1** (max 2-3 cues per moment), **Rule 2** (natural conversational language in the final copy), and especially **Rule 7** (atomic descriptions, never ready-made copy lines — regenerate every time).
