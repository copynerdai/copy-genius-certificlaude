# Big Idea

> Methodology for developing the **Big Idea** — the creative angle of vision from which headline, lead, hook, and the entire argumentation descend.
>
> Used by the **Copy Genius Strategist** during Phase 1 (Discovery), AFTER mass desire is selected, awareness is mapped, sophistication stage is determined, and the Unique Mechanism (if needed) is articulated.
>
> Unlike the other strategic framework files, this one is **not a mechanical pipeline**. The Big Idea is where AI consistently struggles without human input — this is where the *human creative spark* enters the system. The Strategist's role here is to **set boundaries, seed prompts, evaluate proposals, and stress-test candidates** — not to generate finished Big Ideas autonomously. The copywriter discovers; the Strategist guides.
>
> **Consumer contract**
> Read by: the Strategist (with the copywriter as creative partner) — at: Phase 1 (Discovery), Big Idea step, AFTER mass desire, awareness, sophistication, and UM are set.
> Output: fills funnel-brief block §3.7 — [funnel-brief](core/strategic-frameworks/funnel-brief.md) owns the canonical block format; this file only explains how to derive the content.
> Load-bearing sections (always read): §1 (core principle + swap test), §3 (gate check), §4 (criteria + anti-patterns), §5 (the 6 sources, as prompt-seeds), §6 (shaping by awareness/sophistication), §8 (collaborative workflow). Reference (on demand): §2 (why this file differs), §7 (splinter market), §9 (worked conversation), §10 (brief output pointer), §11 (False Big Idea), §12 (cross-references).

## 1. Core principle

A **Big Idea** is the ONE perspective that makes a specific product-market-timing combination unique. It is the angle of vision that makes everything else in the piece feel inevitable.

A real Big Idea must do four things simultaneously:

1. **Channel** an existing mass desire onto the specific product (it never creates desire — `mass-desire.md` already selected the desire to channel)
2. **Create irresistible curiosity** — the prospect must know more
3. **Differentiate immediately** — the idea cannot work for a competitor's product
4. **Sustain the entire piece** from headline to CTA — if energy fades after the lead, it's a hook, not a Big Idea

### The "Big Idea is not…" clarifications

| The Big Idea is NOT… | …but… |
|---|---|
| A headline | The headline *expresses* the Big Idea in compressed form |
| A hook | The hook is the Big Idea's first 3 seconds of expression |
| A USP | The USP is what the product is; the Big Idea is how the prospect sees it |
| A Unique Mechanism | The UM is the proprietary system; the Big Idea is the angle from which the UM is presented |
| A slogan or tagline | A slogan can carry the Big Idea but isn't the Big Idea itself |
| A bigger promise | The promise sets the desire ceiling; the Big Idea reframes how the prospect approaches that promise |

### The hierarchy

```
Big Idea               (angle of vision — internal)
  ↓ generates
Campaign Thesis        (from unique-mechanism.md §8 — documented in the brief's
                        "Campaign thesis" field, funnel-brief §3.4)
  ↓ generates
Headline               (concrete expression of the idea)
  ↓ generates
Lead                   (amplifies the idea)
  ↓ generates
Body / Mechanism / Proof / Offer / CTA
```

### The "swap the product name" test

If you remove your product from the Big Idea and substitute a competitor's product, does the idea still work?

- **Still works** → not connected enough to your UM / product. It's a market observation, not a Big Idea for you.
- **Falls apart** → real Big Idea. Only your product can fulfill the angle.

This test is the single most important gate. Many candidate ideas pass every other check but fail this one and are dropped.

## 2. Why this file is different from the others

The other strategic framework files (`mass-desire.md`, `awareness-levels.md`, `unique-mechanism.md`, `chain-of-beliefs.md`, `proof-elements.md`, `naming.md`) operate on mechanical pipelines: gather inputs → run rules → produce output. Each has an operational sequence the Strategist can execute mostly on its own.

The Big Idea is the **one creative cornerstone of the system** where AI underperforms by itself. Generating "interesting" Big Ideas is the part of copywriting where the human creative spark — pattern recognition across unrelated domains, lived intuition about what stops people, emotional resonance with the avatar — does the bulk of the work.

### The Strategist's role here

The Strategist in this workflow is **not** the source of the Big Idea. The Strategist is:

1. **The boundary-setter** — what kind of Big Idea this funnel needs (by awareness + sophistication + UM context)
2. **The prompt-seeder** — provides scaffolds, observations, and questions the copywriter can build from
3. **The evaluator** — assesses copywriter-proposed Big Ideas against criteria, identifies weaknesses
4. **The stress-tester** — runs swap-the-product, sustains-the-piece, and other validation tests
5. **The iteration partner** — suggests mergers, sharpenings, alternative framings until at least one candidate passes all criteria

### The Strategist is NOT here to

- Generate 2-3 finished Big Ideas from inputs alone (this produces generic, lifeless ideas)
- Close the Big Idea selection autonomously (the copywriter selects — copywriter-led principle applies)
- Pretend the Big Idea is a mechanical synthesis of awareness + UM + mass desire (those inputs CONSTRAIN the territory but they do not generate the angle)

### Operating mode

When invoked, the Strategist enters **brainstorming-partnership mode**:

- Frames the territory
- Asks generative questions
- Evaluates honestly
- Iterates with the copywriter until one candidate is strong
- Then asks the copywriter to select

This is not a one-shot interaction. It's a back-and-forth that may take several rounds.

## 3. Gate check — does this piece need a Big Idea?

Not every piece needs a Big Idea. Forcing one onto an unfit awareness/sophistication combination weakens the message.

### Decision matrix

| Awareness | Big Idea needed? | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Most Aware** | **NO** | The offer IS the idea. Product + price + reason-why + urgency. A Big Idea here is overcreative and dilutes the simplicity. |
| **Product Aware** | **OPTIONAL** | A new angle on a known product. Only run Big Idea development if the direct angles (offer / new proof / new mechanism elaboration) feel exhausted. |
| **Solution Aware** | **YES** | The idea must crystallize the desire AND connect it to *your* mechanism specifically. A Big Idea makes your approach feel categorically different, not incrementally better. |
| **Problem Aware** | **ESSENTIAL** | The prospect must see the problem differently before they will accept a new solution. The Big Idea does that reframing. |
| **Unaware** | **NON-NEGOTIABLE** | Pure identification or curiosity. Without a Big Idea there is nothing to attach the prospect's attention to. This is the hardest and highest-leverage Big Idea to develop. |

### Sophistication amplifier

Within each awareness level, higher sophistication stages increase the demand for a stronger, more original Big Idea:

- **Stage 1-2**: claim-driven. The Big Idea sits on the promise.
- **Stage 3**: the mechanism enters the headline (structural inversion — see §6). The Big Idea often *wraps around* the mechanism rather than the promise.
- **Stage 4**: mechanism elaborated; the Big Idea must differentiate at the proof / refinement layer.
- **Stage 5**: identification replaces mechanism; the Big Idea is pure "this is for people like ME" — the hardest variant.

### When NOT to develop a Big Idea (STOP gate)

If awareness is **Most Aware** AND sophistication is **Stage 1-2**: STOP. Do not run the brainstorming workflow.

Declare to the copywriter:

> *"This combination doesn't require a Big Idea. The direct claim + offer is the strongest approach. Forcing creativity here would damage the message."*

Generating a Big Idea here is not neutral — it actively damages the message by adding unnecessary complexity to a context where simplicity wins.

### Format rule (cross-format coherence)

In a multi-touchpoint funnel, the Big Idea is **ONE**. Every format declines it differently:

- **Long format** (LP, VSL, webinar) → Big Idea expressed in the hook, dimensionalized in the lead, developed across the body
- **Short format** (ad, email, social) → Big Idea compressed into the hook. The hook is not separate from the Big Idea — the hook IS the Big Idea in compressed form

Do not develop different Big Ideas per touchpoint. The funnel's coherence depends on a single angle of vision animating every piece.

## 4. What makes a Big Idea real — the criteria

For any candidate Big Idea (copywriter-proposed or Strategist-seeded), validate against these criteria. These are the evaluation rubric used in §8.

### Mandatory criteria

1. **One-sentence test passed**. The candidate can complete: *"The idea is that ___."* If you can't crystallize it in one sentence, it's not a Big Idea yet — it's a vague direction.
2. **Specificity**. The idea names something concrete, not a generic frame ("it's about transformation" is not a Big Idea; "you're not aging — your cells are dehydrated" is).
3. **Surprise**. The prospect cannot think *"I already knew that"*. The idea reveals something they hadn't seen, contradicts something they believed, or names something they felt but couldn't articulate.
4. **Product-connection**. Only THIS product (or this brand's UM) fulfills the angle. The swap-the-product test passes (§1).
5. **Expandability**. The idea has enough material behind it to sustain 20 minutes of VSL or 8 LP sections without running out. If it exhausts itself in the lead, it's a hook.
6. **Emotional connection**. The idea hooks an emotion (curiosity / outrage / hope / fear / vindication / belonging) — not pure logic. Logic-only ideas don't sustain attention.
7. **Awareness-fit**. The idea matches what the awareness level demands:
   - Problem Aware → reframes the problem
   - Solution Aware → differentiates the solution
   - Product Aware → introduces a new perspective on a known product
   - Unaware → captures through identification or curiosity (never through claims/benefits/problems)
8. **Sophistication-fit**. The idea sits at the right structural position for the stage:
   - Stage 1-2: idea wraps around the promise
   - Stage 3: idea wraps around the mechanism (structural inversion)
   - Stage 5: idea is identification

### Disqualifying anti-patterns

- **Too abstract** ("It's about freedom" — vague, fits any product)
- **Too narrow** (only resonates with a 0.5% sub-segment of the avatar)
- **Already-said** (a competitor or a prior campaign in the category has used this angle — verify via competitor research)
- **Hook-only** (works for the first 30 seconds, fades after — fails the sustains-the-piece test)
- **Logic-only** (no emotional anchor — won't carry through long-form)
- **Implausible** (the proof cannot be assembled to defend the angle — see `proof-elements.md` §2.2 scale principle)

## 5. The 6 sources of Big Ideas (inspiration scaffolds)

These are the six archetypal sources Big Ideas emerge from. In the Strategist-copywriter workflow (§8), the Strategist uses these as **prompt-seeds** — questions to ask the copywriter to spark the creative process — not as templates to fill in.

The strongest Big Ideas combine **2-3 sources**. A single-source Big Idea is acceptable; multi-source ideas are more durable.

### Source 1 — Problem from a new angle

Take the prospect's problem and recontextualize it so their old understanding becomes insufficient.

**Prompt-seeds for the copywriter:**

- *"What does the prospect currently think causes their problem? What if that cause is actually a symptom of something deeper?"*
- *"Where is the prospect looking for the problem? Where SHOULD they be looking?"*
- *"What category does the prospect put this problem in? What if it's actually in a different category?"*

**Pattern (illustrative):** the prospect thinks joint pain is from aging → the Big Idea reframes it as a "drought" inside the cartilage. Suddenly anti-inflammatories are obviously insufficient and a hydration-based mechanism makes sense.

**Pairs naturally with:** Route A (Original Problem) from `unique-mechanism.md` §6 + UMP from `unique-mechanism.md` §7.

### Source 2 — Observable phenomenon

A verifiable, counterintuitive data point or fact that connects directly to the solution.

**Prompt-seeds for the copywriter:**

- *"What's a number in this category that contradicts what most people assume?"*
- *"What's a counterintuitive finding from research, internal data, or customer outcomes that the prospect would find shocking?"*
- *"Is there a hidden correlation between two things the prospect tracks separately?"*

**Pattern (illustrative):** *"94% of deals that lose money share one specific thing — the investor never filled out a single-page pre-deal analysis."* The phenomenon is concrete, contradicts intuition, points directly at the product.

**Pairs naturally with:** Proof Category 3 (empirical) and Cat 1.4 (doing the math) from `proof-elements.md`.

### Source 3 — Powerful analogy

A parallel from a completely different domain that illuminates the prospect's situation in a new light.

**Prompt-seeds for the copywriter:**

- *"What other system in the world has the same dynamics as the prospect's problem? Engineering, biology, history, sport, cooking, navigation, music?"*
- *"What metaphor makes the abstract concrete?"*
- *"What domain does the prospect already understand that I can borrow the structure from?"*

**Pattern (illustrative):** *"Your business is a leaky bucket — you keep pouring water in instead of fixing the holes."* The analogy makes the abstract (cash flow, retention) instantly visceral.

**Pairs naturally with:** Proof Category 1.7 (stories, metaphors, analogies) from `proof-elements.md`.

### Source 4 — Emblematic story

A true story (founder, customer, or contextual) that embodies the message and becomes the campaign's center of gravity.

**Prompt-seeds for the copywriter:**

- *"Whose specific story, if told well, would make the prospect feel 'this is about me'?"*
- *"What single customer transformation captures the entire promise in one arc?"*
- *"Is there a founder moment — a personal struggle, a discovery, a turning point — that contains the entire pitch in one episode?"*

**Pattern (illustrative):** *"Marco did 14 deals. 11 lost money. Then he changed ONE thing in his process — and the next 30 deals all profited."* The story is the angle; the mechanism reveals itself inside the story.

**Pairs naturally with:** Proof Category 1.7 (stories), Proof Category 5.2 (case studies), and brand belief B6 (founder discovered through struggle) from `chain-of-beliefs.md`.

### Source 5 — Enemy / conspiracy

Name an external entity that's preventing the prospect from getting what they want. Never the prospect themselves — always something outside.

**Prompt-seeds for the copywriter:**

- *"Who or what is profiting from the prospect's problem staying unsolved?"*
- *"Which industry, lobby, legacy institution, or established expert has an incentive to keep the prospect confused?"*
- *"What 'truth' has been installed in the market that benefits someone other than the prospect?"*

**Pattern (illustrative):** *"The industry teaches you to find deals, but never how to EVALUATE them — because evaluation training would make you stop buying their courses."* The enemy frame creates outrage and instant alignment.

**Caution**: the enemy must be DEFENSIBLE. If the conspiracy claim doesn't survive scrutiny, the entire campaign collapses on B4 (brand trustworthy). Use only when the enemy is real and the brand can defend the claim with proof.

**Pairs naturally with:** the third contradiction technique (conflict of interest / disinformation) from `chain-of-beliefs.md` §8.

### Source 6 — Counterintuitive revelation

A direct contradiction of a belief the prospect holds firmly. The reverse of the contrarian naming technique (`naming.md` Technique 5), applied to the angle rather than the name.

**Prompt-seeds for the copywriter:**

- *"What's the most widespread belief in this category? What if the opposite is true — and you can prove it?"*
- *"What does the prospect 'just know' that turns out to be wrong?"*
- *"What sounds like common sense in the category but is actually the cause of the prospect's failure?"*

**Pattern (illustrative):** *"The more properties you look at, the MORE money you lose."* Directly contradicts the prospect's instinct (more research = better decisions). The reveal then explains why it's true.

**Caution**: the contradiction must be defensible. Manufactured contrarianism (saying the opposite for shock value without substance) damages B4 and is a False Big Idea (§7).

**Pairs naturally with:** Route A (Original Problem) from `unique-mechanism.md` §6.

### Combining sources

Strong Big Ideas operate on 2-3 sources simultaneously. Examples of common combinations:

| Combination | Effect |
|---|---|
| Source 1 (new angle) + Source 6 (counterintuitive) | The prospect's problem is reframed AND their current belief about it is contradicted. Heavy persuasive load. |
| Source 4 (emblematic story) + Source 5 (enemy) | The story shows how the protagonist was failed by [enemy] until they discovered [the truth]. Classic founder-or-customer hero narrative. |
| Source 2 (phenomenon) + Source 3 (analogy) | A surprising data point is made viscerally understandable through an analogy from a familiar domain. |
| Source 4 (story) + Source 1 (new angle) | A specific person's story embodies the larger reframing of the problem. Carries identification AND mechanism in one. |

When the Strategist is helping the copywriter brainstorm, suggesting a combination is often more productive than suggesting a single source.

## 6. Shaping by awareness and sophistication

The awareness level and sophistication stage shape what the Big Idea must DO. Use this section to set the territory before brainstorming.

### Problem Aware → reframe the problem

The Big Idea must make the prospect think: *"I've been looking at this wrong."*

- Reveal a new mechanism behind the pain
- Expose a hidden factor
- Contradict a belief about the cause
- Open cognitive space for a new solution

**Source fit**: Source 1 (new angle) and Source 6 (counterintuitive) are the default scaffolds. Source 4 (emblematic story) works when the brand has a strong founder or customer narrative.

**Connection to UM**: heavy reliance on UMP (`unique-mechanism.md` §7) — the UMP installs the reframed cause; the Big Idea is the angle from which the UMP is presented.

### Solution Aware → differentiate the solution

The Big Idea must make YOUR approach feel categorically different — not incrementally better.

- The category works (the prospect already believes this)
- Other approaches within the category have failed them
- YOUR approach is different at a fundamental level, not at the edges

**Source fit**: Source 1 (new angle on the WHY this category works), Source 2 (observable phenomenon supporting your specific approach), Source 5 (enemy = the category-default that fails most people).

**Connection to UM**: heavy reliance on UMS (`unique-mechanism.md` §7) — the UMS shows comparative superiority within the category; the Big Idea is the angle that makes the superiority feel categorical.

### Product Aware → new angle on a known product

The prospect knows the product. The Big Idea provides a fresh perspective that breaks pattern recognition.

**Source fit**: any of the 6 sources can work; Source 2 (new phenomenon / proof) and Source 4 (new story) are common in mature campaigns refreshing tired product copy.

**Connection to the 5 Attack Angles** (Schwartz, Product Aware — the full angle table with when-to-use / when-to-avoid lives in the Product Aware section of [awareness-levels](core/strategic-frameworks/awareness-levels.md)): the Big Idea informs which Attack Angle to deploy. The chosen angle is written into the "Attack angle (Product Aware only)" field of the brief's Big Idea block (funnel-brief §3.7):

1. New mental image of desire fulfilled (Big Idea pulls from Source 3 analogy or Source 4 story)
2. Sharpen the idea of how the product satisfies the desire (Big Idea pulls from Source 2 phenomenon)
3. Expand perception of where/when the product satisfies (Big Idea pulls from Source 1 new angle, especially for splinter markets — see §7)
4. New proof / details / documentation (Big Idea pulls from Source 2 phenomenon)
5. Present the Unique Mechanism (Big Idea pulls from Source 1 + Source 6)

### Unaware → identification or curiosity

The Big Idea must capture attention without referencing the product, the problem, the solution, the price, the function, or the desire. The prospect doesn't know they have a problem — anything that triggers their pattern recognition for "marketing" loses them.

**Source fit**: Source 4 (emblematic story) and Source 3 (powerful analogy) are the workhorses. Source 6 (counterintuitive revelation) works when the contradiction is sufficiently abstracted from the eventual product pitch.

**Critical rule**: the Big Idea must echo an emotion, attitude, or identity that picks the right people out of the crowd. The lead then unfolds: identification → growing problem awareness → solution exists → your product. The Big Idea is what the prospect identifies WITH before they identify the problem.

### Most Aware → typically no Big Idea (see §3 STOP gate)

If a Big Idea is being developed for Most Aware (rare exception, e.g., a re-engagement campaign for a dormant warm list), it sits in the realm of *renewed urgency* or *new mechanism elaboration* — but generally the direct claim + offer wins.

### Sophistication-specific: the Structural Inversion at Stage 3+

At Stage 3, a critical inversion happens in the headline structure:

- **Stage 1-2**: the headline is ALL claim. The mechanism (if mentioned) is subordinate.
- **Stage 3**: the arrangement REVERSES. The basic claim has become well-known — perhaps too well-known. The claim can be applied to itself as shorthand (one word, implied), and the headline becomes devoted to explaining HOW the claim is accomplished — the mechanism.

**Implication for the Big Idea**: at Stage 3+, the Big Idea wraps around the MECHANISM, not the promise. The mechanism is brought into the headline to establish the point of difference; the claim is restated in full only once the mechanism has earned the prospect's attention.

**Stage 5 special case**: the field is exhausted of mechanisms. The Big Idea becomes pure identification — "we are YOUR people". No claims, no mechanisms; the prospect buys because they identify with the brand's personality. This is the hardest Big Idea archetype and almost always requires Source 4 (emblematic story / founder identity) as its center of gravity.

## 7. Splinter Market opportunity

When the main market is saturated (Stage 4-5 sophistication, or the main desire is over-served), look for sub-segments where the **same product satisfies a DIFFERENT desire**. Targeting the splinter creates a virgin market (Stage 1 sophistication) inside an exhausted one.

### Splinter discovery questions

- *"What sub-groups want this result for a DIFFERENT reason than the main avatar?"*
- *"What adjacent markets have the same problem but aren't being addressed by the category?"*
- *"What smaller, more specific version of the mass desire is nobody targeting yet?"*

### Example pattern

A general fitness program → main market: weight loss → saturated, Stage 5. Splinter: women in perimenopause whose weight gain has a hormonal cause → the SAME program now satisfies a DIFFERENT (more specific) desire → Stage 1 sophistication for the splinter, no competitors speaking specifically to them yet.

The Big Idea for the splinter is essentially Source 1 (new angle): *"Your weight gain isn't from your diet — it's from your hormones. And the hormones won't respond to a generic program."* The product is the same; the angle of vision is calibrated to a niche the main market hasn't touched.

### When to deploy splinter strategy

- Main market awareness is Most Aware on the dominant desire (offer competition dominates)
- Main market sophistication is Stage 4-5 (mechanisms exhausted)
- The brand can credibly serve the splinter (the product genuinely fits the splinter's specific needs)
- The brand has the data or domain knowledge to differentiate for the splinter

### Cross-reference

The Splinter Market choice is a copywriter-led decision (per the copywriter-led principle). The Strategist surfaces the splinter opportunity if signals from competitor research suggest the main market is exhausted; the copywriter decides whether to pursue the splinter or stay on the main market.

## 8. The collaborative brainstorming workflow

This is the operational loop the Strategist runs when invoked to develop a Big Idea. The flow is **bidirectional** — the copywriter proposes, the Strategist evaluates, both iterate until at least one candidate is strong.

### Step 1 — Strategist gathers inputs

Before engaging the copywriter, the Strategist reads:

- **Awareness level** (from `awareness-levels.md` output — already in the brief)
- **Sophistication stage** (from `unique-mechanism.md` §2 output)
- **Mass desire** (from `mass-desire.md` output, including the dominant dimension)
- **Unique Mechanism + UMP/UMS + side (Route A/B)** (from `unique-mechanism.md` output)
- **Avatar materials** (from `brands/<brand>/avatars/*.md` — lived experience, current beliefs, mental dialogue, identity markers)
- **Customer transcripts and reviews** (from `brands/<brand>/transcripts/` and `brands/<brand>/testimonials.md` — for emergent angles)
- **Competitor messaging** (from `brands/<brand>/competitors/*.md` — to know which angles are already taken)
- **Brand's founder story and positioning** (from `brands/<brand>/brand.md` — for Source 4 material)

### Step 2 — Strategist runs the gate check

Decide: does this piece need a Big Idea? (§3)

- If **NO** (Most Aware + Stage 1-2): declare it explicitly to the copywriter and STOP. No brainstorm.
- If **YES** (Solution / Problem / Unaware) or **YES if direct angle exhausted** (Product Aware): proceed to Step 3.

### Step 3 — Strategist frames the territory (the brief-back)

The Strategist sends a brief-back to the copywriter that defines:

- **What the Big Idea must DO** for this awareness + sophistication combination (from §6)
- **Which sources fit best** for this context (from §5 — recommend 2-3 source archetypes, not all 6)
- **Which sources are CLOSED** because competitors already use them (from competitor research)
- **What kind of emotional anchor** the avatar will respond to (from avatar materials)
- **What proof inventory is available** to back up potential angles (from `proof-elements.md` output — keeps the scale principle alive: don't develop a Big Idea that the proof can't sustain)
- **Constraints**: language, format, length expectations, splinter market option (if relevant)

This brief-back is the Strategist's only "monologue" in the workflow. From here on it's dialogue.

### Step 4 — Strategist seeds (optional, only if copywriter requests)

If the copywriter is starting cold or wants seeds, the Strategist can offer:

- **2-3 prompt-questions** drawn from the recommended sources (§5). Each is a question, not a finished idea.
- **1-2 angles observed in adjacent industries** that could be ported here
- **1-2 specific phrases from customer transcripts** that could become the seed of an angle (without forcing them into Big Ideas themselves)

**Critical**: the Strategist never offers a finished "The idea is that ___" as a seed. Seeds are raw material; the copywriter does the synthesis. Pushing finished ideas at this stage compromises the copywriter's creative ownership and tends to produce generic results.

### Step 5 — Copywriter proposes 1-3 candidates

The copywriter brings candidate Big Ideas, each phrased as a complete sentence:

> *"The idea is that ___."*

Plus optional notes: which source(s) the candidate draws from, why this angle, what concern the copywriter has about it.

### Step 6 — Strategist evaluates each candidate

For each candidate, the Strategist runs the **criteria check** from §4:

1. One-sentence test — passed?
2. Specificity — concrete or vague?
3. Surprise — does the prospect think "I already knew that"?
4. Product-connection — does the swap-the-product test pass?
5. Expandability — sustains 20 minutes of material?
6. Emotional connection — what emotion is anchored?
7. Awareness-fit — matches what this awareness level demands?
8. Sophistication-fit — sits at the right structural position?

And checks for **anti-patterns** (§4):

- Too abstract?
- Too narrow?
- Already-said in the category?
- Hook-only?
- Logic-only?
- Implausible (proof can't sustain it)?

### Step 7 — Strategist runs stress tests on the strongest candidate(s)

For any candidate that survives Step 6, run:

**Test A — Swap the product name** (§1)

Remove the brand's product from the Big Idea, substitute a competitor's product. Does it still work?
- *Works* → the idea is not connected enough; iterate.
- *Falls apart* → real Big Idea.

**Test B — Sustains the piece**

Mentally walk through: headline → lead → mechanism section → proof section → offer → CTA. Does the Big Idea power each section, or does it run out of fuel after the lead?
- *Fades after lead* → it's a hook, not a Big Idea. Either accept that (some funnels need only a hook) or iterate.
- *Powers every section* → real Big Idea.

**Test C — Expandable to 20+ minutes**

Can the Big Idea generate 20+ minutes of material (or 8+ LP sections)? Is there enough behind it — story arcs, proof angles, mechanism layers, objection-handling — to fill long-form?
- *Runs out quickly* → idea is too thin; iterate.
- *Has depth* → real Big Idea.

**Test D — The "tell me more" test**

After hearing the Big Idea in one sentence, does the prospect want to know more? Or do they nod and move on?
- *Move on* → no irresistible curiosity. Iterate.
- *Want more* → real Big Idea.

**Test E — Defensibility**

Especially for Source 5 (enemy) and Source 6 (counterintuitive): can the brand defend the claim with proof?
- *Cannot defend* → the Big Idea will collapse on contact with scrutiny. Iterate.
- *Can defend* → proceed.

### Step 8 — Strategist returns evaluation to the copywriter

For each candidate, the Strategist returns:

- **Criteria score**: how many of the 8 mandatory criteria pass
- **Anti-patterns flagged**: which (if any) apply
- **Stress test results**: A through E
- **Specific weaknesses**: precise, actionable notes
- **Suggested refinements**: how to sharpen the candidate (mergers with other sources, tighter framing, alternative phrasings)

### Step 9 — Iteration loop

The copywriter revises, mergers candidates, drops weak ones, develops new ones based on the Strategist's notes. The Strategist re-evaluates. The loop repeats until at least one candidate passes:

- All 8 mandatory criteria
- No anti-patterns
- Tests A-E

This loop typically runs 2-4 cycles. If the loop exceeds 5 cycles without convergence, the Strategist surfaces this to the copywriter: maybe the underlying inputs (awareness selection, UM, mass desire) need re-examination, or maybe a Big Idea isn't actually possible at this stage (rare, but it happens — usually means the product is too commoditized or the mass desire is too saturated).

### Step 10 — Copywriter selects

From the validated candidates, the copywriter picks one. The Strategist may recommend with rationale (e.g., *"Candidate 2 has stronger emotional anchor; Candidate 3 is more defensible. For a VSL where emotional sustain matters more, I'd recommend Candidate 2"*), but the copywriter selects.

### Step 11 — Strategist documents in the brief

The selected Big Idea is locked into the brief's §3.7 block (see §10 for the field map). Two sub-steps:

1. **Fill the core fields** of funnel-brief §3.7 from the workflow's outputs (idea sentence, rationale, structure, optional fields).
2. **Derive the Big Idea expressions across the funnel** — WITH the copywriter, derive one expression per touchpoint declared in the funnel architecture (ad / advertorial / LP hero / VSL lead / email subject patterns / other). Each expression is the same Big Idea declined for that touchpoint's format (per the §3 format rule: ONE idea, many declensions). These expressions are the field every downstream specialist consumes — the brief is not complete without them.

## 9. Worked example — a brainstorming conversation (illustrative)

This is what the workflow looks like in practice. Illustrative only.

**Context**: Problem Aware, Stage 3, mass desire = sleep quality, UM = nervous-system regulation protocol (Type B Hidden), Route A (Original Problem).

---

**Strategist (Step 3 — frames territory):**

> The Big Idea here must reframe the prospect's understanding of why they're not sleeping. They believe it's stress, screens, age, hormones — the standard explanations. Sources 1 (new angle) and 6 (counterintuitive) are the strongest fits. Source 4 (founder story) is available — the founder has a documented chronic insomnia recovery that took 4 years. Source 5 (enemy) is risky here — the sleep-aid industry is an obvious target but the contradiction is hard to defend without the proof inventory we currently have.
>
> Proof available: 1 founder transformation story (deep), 1 published research paper on nervous-system regulation, 8 strong customer testimonials with specific outcomes, no industry-conspiracy proof.
>
> Avoid: "your sleep is broken because of your screens / your stress / your hormones / your age" — all four of those angles are already in the category. The competitor wiki shows 6 brands using the cortisol angle and 4 brands using the screen-blue-light angle.

**Copywriter (Step 5 — proposes 2 candidates):**

> Candidate A: *"The idea is that your sleep isn't broken — your nervous system is stuck in 'on' mode, and no pillow / app / supplement / habit can fix that until you address the lock itself."*
>
> Candidate B: *"The idea is that the more you try to sleep, the harder it gets — because trying activates the very system that prevents sleep."*

**Strategist (Step 6 — evaluates):**

> **Candidate A**:
> - One-sentence test: ✓ passes
> - Specificity: ✓ specific (names the "lock", the "on mode")
> - Surprise: ✓ contradicts the dominant frame (it's not your habits; it's your nervous system architecture)
> - Product-connection: ✓ passes the swap-the-product test — only your UM (nervous-system regulation) addresses the lock specifically. A competitor selling melatonin or a sleep app couldn't claim this angle.
> - Expandability: ✓ deep — mechanism layer (how the lock works) + proof layer (research + testimonials) + story layer (founder's 4-year recovery)
> - Emotional connection: ✓ vindication — the prospect is told "your past failures weren't your fault" (chain-of-beliefs #8)
> - Awareness-fit (Problem Aware): ✓ reframes the problem cleanly
> - Sophistication-fit (Stage 3): ✓ wraps around the mechanism, not the promise
> - **Sources combined**: 1 (new angle) + 6 (counterintuitive)
> - **Stress tests A-E**: all pass
>
> **Candidate B**:
> - One-sentence test: ✓ passes
> - Specificity: weaker — "the very system" is vague
> - Surprise: ✓ counterintuitive
> - Product-connection: weaker — the angle "trying makes it worse" doesn't uniquely require your UM. A meditation app, a hypnosis program, a passive-relaxation protocol could all claim it.
> - Sustains the piece: weaker — runs out after the lead. The "trying harder makes it worse" frame doesn't carry into the mechanism section the way Candidate A does.
> - **Stress test A (swap product)**: FAILS — the idea works for too many alternatives.
> - **Recommendation**: Candidate B is a strong hook but a weak Big Idea. It would carry the ad and the lead, then leave the body without fuel.

**Strategist returns**: Candidate A is the stronger Big Idea. Candidate B is a strong hook that could be the opening 30 seconds of the VSL but cannot drive the whole piece. Recommendation: lock Candidate A as the Big Idea; consider Candidate B's phrasing as a candidate for the hook within Candidate A's umbrella.

**Copywriter (Step 10 — selects)**: Candidate A. Approved.

**Strategist (Step 11 — documents)**: Big Idea locked into brief.

## 10. Output into the funnel brief

The Big Idea block is locked into the funnel brief after the brainstorming workflow converges.

### Format — pointer

Fill the brief's Big Idea block — §3.7 of [funnel-brief](core/strategic-frameworks/funnel-brief.md), which owns the canonical block format. Do NOT redefine the block here. How to fill each §3.7 field from this workflow:

- **Big Idea name** — a short memorable handle, if the idea has one (invoke `naming.md` only if a campaign-bearing name is needed)
- **The Big Idea in one sentence** — the validated "The idea is that ___" completion the copywriter selected at Step 10
- **Why this idea (over alternatives)** — synthesize from the Step 6-8 evaluation: criteria score, sources used (§5), awareness-fit and sophistication-fit (§6), and the strongest stress-test dimension
- **Idea structure → Hook angle** — the hook expression developed during the workflow (the Big Idea's compressed opening; later input to the hook-specialist)
- **Idea structure → Promise embedded** — what the idea promises before any feature is mentioned
- **Idea structure → Mechanism it implies** — how the idea attaches to UMP and/or UMS (the "Connection to UM" reasoning from §6)
- **Idea structure → Emotional charge** — the emotion anchored per criterion 6 of §4
- **Attack angle (Product Aware only)** — the chosen Schwartz attack angle from §6's Product Aware mapping; "n/a" at other awareness levels
- **Sustaining material (optional field in §3.7)** — the bullet list of what the idea unfolds into across the body: proof anchors, mechanism layer, story beats, objection-handling angles. Produced by this workflow (Test C material); record it so long-form specialists inherit it.
- **Known weaknesses (optional field in §3.7)** — any weakness the copywriter accepted with awareness (e.g., "weaker on emotional vindication; reinforced via founder story in section 4")
- **Big Idea expressions across the funnel** — one expression per declared touchpoint, derived with the copywriter at Step 11 sub-step 2
- **False Big Idea check** — Pass / Fail per §11 of this file

Workflow-record extras worth annotating as notes inside the block (not canonical fields): brainstorm rounds taken; splinter market deployment (yes/no, sub-segment named).

## 11. False Big Idea — the trap

A False Big Idea has the shape of an angle of vision but none of the substance. It looks like creative work but performs none of the persuasive work.

### Symptoms

- **Hook-only**: the idea is interesting for 30 seconds and runs out. Fails Test B (sustains the piece). Often happens when the idea is essentially a clever line, not an angle of vision.
- **Abstract**: the idea is too generic to commit to anything specific. "It's about transformation" / "It's about freedom" / "It's about becoming who you were meant to be" — fits any product in any category.
- **Already-said**: a competitor or a prior campaign in the category has used this angle. The prospect's pattern recognition for "this angle" is already saturated. Verify against competitor research before locking.
- **Unsupportable**: the proof inventory cannot defend the angle. Common with Source 5 (enemy) — the conspiracy claim sounds compelling but the brand has no evidence to back it.
- **Logic-only**: the idea is a clever logical construction with no emotional anchor. Survives Test D ("tell me more") for analytical prospects but fails for the broader market.
- **Product-disconnected**: the idea is interesting and emotionally resonant but doesn't require this specific product. Fails Test A.

### Why False Big Ideas are worse than no Big Idea

A False Big Idea consumes the copywriter's creative budget and the funnel's attention budget without delivering persuasion. The piece reads as "creative" but converts as "noise". Worse, the copywriter and brand both invest in the idea (production, ad spend, copy investment) before discovering the angle doesn't carry.

### How to avoid

- Run the criteria checklist (§4) without shortcuts
- Run all five stress tests (Steps 7A-E)
- If a candidate fails Test A (swap the product), kill it — don't try to salvage. A non-product-connected idea is fundamentally a market observation, not a Big Idea for THIS brand.
- If a candidate fails Test C (expandable), accept it as a hook (not a Big Idea) and continue brainstorming

### Cross-reference to the False family

The canonical list of the False traps lives in [unique-mechanism](core/strategic-frameworks/unique-mechanism.md) §5 — this file does not re-list it. False Big Idea (this section) is the creative-angle member of that family: angle shape without depth. All traps in the family share the same failure mode — surface-level shape without underlying substance — and the defense in each case is the criteria checklist + the stress tests, run rigorously and without shortcuts.
