# Persuasion Techniques — Diagnostic Decision Tree

> Foundational strategic framework. The 7 techniques of *Breakthrough Advertising* are NOT a toolkit to apply in parallel. They are **responses to specific symptoms in the prospect's mental state** at specific points in the copy.
>
> When writing, the rule is inverted from the old "apply every technique to every paragraph" approach:
>
> 1. Read the prospect's likely mental state at this point in the copy
> 2. Identify the symptom (or absence of symptom)
> 3. Activate ONLY the technique that responds to that symptom
> 4. If no symptom is present → no technique is needed at this point
>

## 1. Core principle

A persuasion technique is a **response**, not a default. Schwartz's framework treats the prospect's mind as a sequence of states that the copy walks through. At each state, certain techniques are appropriate and certain ones are not.

The wrong way to use the 7 techniques: take each one, list its sub-methods, then check them against every paragraph. This produces copy where every technique is partially present, none fully deployed, and the prospect feels manipulated rather than persuaded.

The right way: identify what the prospect is feeling at THIS point — vague longing, skepticism toward a claim, resistance from a pre-existing belief, indifference to the differentiator — and deploy the matching technique. If no symptom is present, write the next paragraph clean.

### Schwartz's master rule

> *"Belief depends on structure. Desire depends on promise. Every choice in copy is a choice about preparation: what does this prospect already accept, and what's the next step he can take without breaking?"*

The decision tree below is a decision tree about **preparation**. Each technique is the preparation appropriate to a specific obstacle.

### The single diagnostic question

When reviewing any section of copy:

> *"What is the prospect feeling here? What's his next-step resistance? What technique answers that specific resistance?"*

If the question has no clear answer, no technique is needed at this point. Write the next paragraph and let the copy breathe.

## 2. The 3 dimensions of the prospect (Schwartz Ch. 6)

Schwartz's foundational claim: body copy works by altering one or more of three dimensions in the prospect's mental world. The 7 techniques map to these dimensions.

| Dimension | What it is | Schwartz's note | Technique that operates on it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Desires** | Physical / material / sensual wants | "Already exist. You cannot create them, only expand them, sharpen them, channel them, give them a goal." | **Intensification** |
| **Identifications** | Roles the prospect wants to play — character roles, achievement roles | "Not material — about expression and recognition. Constant search for self-definition." | **Identification** |
| **Beliefs** | Accepted facts, prejudices, conceptions of reality | "Cannot be smashed head-on. Must be exploited. Immutable, hard as rock." | **Gradualization, Redefinition, Mechanization, Concentration, Camouflage** |

Schwartz then states the three structural questions a copywriter asks for any piece:

1. How much copy is needed to **build desire** to its peak? → Intensification
2. How much to make the prospect feel **comfortable / complimented** by the product, to visualize it in his life structure? → Identification
3. How much to make him **believe** what you've said, compensating for his prejudices? → Gradualization + the four belief mechanisms

The dimensions are not equal weight for every piece. A mass-desire product may need 80% Intensification + 20% belief work. An identity-driven product (luxury, status, lifestyle) may need 60% Identification + 30% Intensification. A category-disruptor in a saturated market may need 70% belief mechanisms + 20% Intensification.

The brief's awareness level and sophistication stage determine the weighting before any technique is selected.

## 3. The diagnostic decision tree — the 7 techniques

For each technique:

- **Prospect symptom** — the mental state Schwartz says the technique is responding to
- **Activate when** — concrete trigger conditions
- **What it does** — the outcome (not the mechanics)
- **Method overview** — high-level operation (NOT a checklist of sub-techniques)
- **Position in copy** — where it sits structurally
- **Stacks with** — compatible techniques
- **Excludes / Avoid when** — conditions where this technique is wrong
- **Wrong-technique warning** — Schwartz's verbatim warnings about what fails

---

### Three operational levels — which techniques live where

Before reading each technique's diagnostic entry, hold this classification. The 7 techniques do NOT all operate at the same level of the piece. Three operational levels distinguish them:

| Level | Techniques | Where they live | Where the Strategist assigns them in the brief |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Structural / global** (wraps the whole piece) | **Camouflage** (orthogonal — voice, format, genre frame) | Decided once for the whole piece — wraps every paragraph | `funnel-brief.md` §4.2 / §4.3 "Piece-level persuasion architecture" block. Determined by brand voice + audience + awareness. |
| **Architectural / container** (the spine of the argumentation) | **Gradualization** (the chain of acceptable steps IS the gradualization) | Coincides with the chain of belief itself — each ring is one acceptance step | Implicit when the chain is built. Not assigned to a single ring; the chain's order in `funnel-brief.md` §3.8 IS the gradualization. |
| **Tactical / per-beat** (operate on specific sections) | **Mechanization**, **Redefinition**, **Concentration**, plus targeted **Intensification spikes**, plus targeted **Identification spikes** | Operate on one specific ring or section, in response to a specific prospect symptom at that point | `funnel-brief.md` §3.8 — each ring's "Persuasion technique" field. One primary tactical technique per ring. |

**Operational implication for the Strategist and the specialists:**

- When you build the chain, you build the **Gradualization architecture** automatically — no separate technique to assign for it.
- When you assign a technique to a single ring (in the brief's §3.8 ring template), pick from the **tactical** layer.
- When you decide the piece's framing (in the brief's §4.2 / §4.3 Piece-level persuasion architecture block), you're choosing the **structural** layer (Camouflage frame) plus whether the **continuous** techniques (Intensification and Identification) run ON or OFF in the body.

**Intensification and Identification** straddle two levels: they can run continuously through the body (architectural-ish) AND can have spikes assigned to specific rings (tactical). The brief captures both: continuous on/off in §4.2 Piece-level architecture; spikes in §3.8 per ring.

### 3.1 INTENSIFICATION

**Prospect symptom**:
The desire is present but **vague, blurry, unfocused**. The prospect feels something — but doesn't see every way the desire can be fulfilled. He hasn't been made to realize "everything he's now leaving behind him."

Schwartz: *"Desires exist as 'blurs — hazy, ambiguous, not yet crystallized into words or images' with 'only a fraction of their true potential power.'"*

**Activate when**:
- The mass desire has been identified but is still unfocused in the prospect's mind
- The copy has space to walk around the product and present it from multiple angles
- The prospect is leaning in but isn't yet compelled to action
- You need to magnify a known desire to overcome skepticism, lethargy, or price resistance

**What it does**:
Strengthens and channels a desire that already exists by presenting it through a series of **fresh, non-repetitive fulfillment images**.

**Method overview** (high-level — NOT a 13-step checklist):
- "Walk around the product in your imagination" — present it from every way it enters the prospect's life
- Each angle must be **fresh** — never repeat the same image with rewording
- The reader must visualize, feel, smell, touch, taste the result
- Repetition is forbidden; **reinforcement through new perspective** is the principle
- Closing often uses Intensification as summary (the guarantee can become a final intensification of the desire)

**Position in copy**:
Body principal (post-lead). Closes may use Intensification as final image-summary. Can also be present in the lead when desire-led structure is appropriate (Solution Aware audiences responding to desire amplification).

**Stacks with**:
- **Identification** — always (they operate on different dimensions: desire vs. role)
- Foundational for all the belief techniques — Intensification produces the desire that the belief techniques then make credible

**Typically installs (chain rings)** — see [chain-of-beliefs](core/strategic-frameworks/chain-of-beliefs.md):
- **Ring 4** (procrastination): #15 (results worth cost), #20 (now or never — penalties of waiting), #3 (problem worsening), #7 (cost of problem exceeds price)
- **Ring 3** support: #18 (brand's UM produces a vivid result), #22 (superior to alternatives — visible/visceral)
- **Ring 1** support: when the prospect's lived experience is intensified to "they're talking about me"

**Excludes / Avoid when**:
- The prospect's resistance is **belief-based, not desire-based** — pure Intensification won't move someone who doesn't believe the claim
- Power-claim Intensification in the headline is wrong when belief barriers exceed desire barriers (then Gradualization opens instead)
- Most Aware retargeting where desire is already focused — don't re-amplify what's locked in

**Wrong-technique warning** (Schwartz):
- *"You cannot repeat, but you can reinforce."* Repeating the same image with reworded language loses the reader mid-ad.
- *"If the additional perspective is not different or dramatic enough to renew your prospect's interest in your claims, then leave it out."*
- Intensification without belief work produces strong desire without conviction — the prospect wants it but doesn't believe he can have it.

### 3.2 IDENTIFICATION

**Prospect symptom**:
The prospect's functional desires are largely satisfied (or the product's functional satisfactions have been exhausted as a sales argument). What he wants now is **expression and recognition** — to define himself to the world, to play a role he aspires to.

Schwartz: *"Above all, in this constant search for self-definition... the desire for self-definition that is subtle, partly unconscious, longing not for satisfaction, but for expression."*

**Activate when**:
- The product does the same functional job as competitors at comparable price → the choice depends on role-difference
- The category includes purchases that "cannot be understood in terms of function alone" (Schwartz says "at least half of all purchases")
- Sophistication is mature — everyone has the same functional claim, identity is the open frontier
- The product has a neutral or negative primary image and needs reframing through role

**What it does**:
Builds a saleable personality into the product so it serves the prospect's longing for expression — turns the product into a vehicle the prospect uses to define himself.

**Method overview**:
- **ONE dominant role** — multiple competing identities cancel each other out
- Character roles (a personality the prospect can wear) OR achievement roles (a status the prospect aspires to)
- **Weave identity throughout** — never label a section "this is your identity." Build it through illustration, mood, situations, frontman behavior
- The product's primary image must remain the **base** — identity is built on top, not as a replacement
- Visual + symbolic layer carries identity more than the literal claim

**Position in copy**:
**Atmospheric — through the entire piece.** Not a section; a quality of the writing. Especially carried by openings, frontman voice, illustrative passages, and how the offer is positioned.

**Stacks with**:
- **Intensification** — always (different dimensions)
- **Camouflage** — the voice can carry identification implicitly through tone choices

**Typically installs (chain rings)** — see [chain-of-beliefs](core/strategic-frameworks/chain-of-beliefs.md):
- **Ring 1** (brand understands my problem): B1 (brand understands), through detailed identification of the prospect's lived experience
- **Brand beliefs**: B6 (founder discovered through struggle — character role), B5 (brand cares — relational identification)
- **Ring 3** support: #12 (works for people like me), #14 (fits my life), #16 (I deserve this), #19 (brand reflects my values), #23 (others will approve)
- Carries identification through the entire piece as atmosphere, not by installing a single ring

**Excludes / Avoid when**:
- The product is purely functional and the prospect buys on functional terms alone (commodity B2B inputs, replacement parts, etc.)
- The identity claim would force the prospect across a believability gap (e.g., mass-market product trying to project elite identity)

**Wrong-technique warning** (Schwartz):
- *"You cannot force your market to accept an unrealistic identification."*
- *"People just won't believe that a product is what it isn't. You cannot contradict accepted images or beliefs in advertising."*
- *"If you demand that your prospect jump across a believability-chasm, your ad will fail."*
- Historical example: the deodorant-soap that used an elite society image to sell mass market — "the disbelief spread to the performance claims themselves, and killed the sales."

Must always include the **primary image as base**. Discard it entirely and the bridge collapses.

### 3.3 GRADUALIZATION

**Prospect symptom**:
The prospect's **pre-existing beliefs** contradict the goal-claim. If the claim is stated raw, he rejects it — not because he doesn't want it, but because his mental world says it can't be true / can't apply to him / can't work.

Schwartz: *"The prospect would feel 'I won't believe it' rather than 'I don't want it.'"*

**Activate when**:
- The strongest possible claim, used as headline, has been tested and **failed** (the prospect rejects the implicit premise)
- The market is at Sophistication Stage 5 or near it — willingness to believe matters more than the promise itself
- Pre-existing beliefs (formed by past failures, category messaging, cultural assumptions) would block the claim if confronted directly
- Skepticism dominates the audience's response to claims in this category

**What it does**:
Builds the **architecture of belief** by starting with what the prospect already accepts and leading him through a chain of acceptances to claims he would have rejected raw.

**Method overview**:
- Identify what the prospect **already believes** — this is the starting platform
- Chain forward step by step — each step must be acceptable on its own and follow from the previous
- Devices Schwartz names: inclusion questions (yes-questions), detailed identification (precision of symptom-description that earns "they're talking about me"), contradiction of false beliefs (gently — never head-on), logical language (if/then chains), syllogistic thinking (the prospect reaches the conclusion himself)
- **Never demand a jump** — the next step must be reachable from where the prospect currently stands

**Position in copy**:
**Structural — it determines the entire architecture of the piece, not a section.** Begins at the lead, runs through the body, closes only when the prospect has accepted the goal-claim. It is the architecture itself.

Schwartz: *"Gradualization determines — not the content of your ad — but its structure, its architecture, the way you build it."*

**Stacks with**:
- **Redefinition** — sits inside the chain to handle specific objections that arise during gradualization
- **Mechanization** — proves a step of the chain that the prospect resists
- **Concentration** — a chain step may be "the existing alternatives fail because..."
- Foundational for all belief work

**Typically installs (chain rings)** — see [chain-of-beliefs](core/strategic-frameworks/chain-of-beliefs.md):
- **Architecture-level** — Gradualization doesn't install one specific ring. It is the **structural architecture** through which all the other techniques install rings in sequence
- Primary deployment surface: **contradicts blocking beliefs** (chain-of-beliefs §8) — counter-example technique
- **Ring 2** (real cause is X): #5, #6, #8 — the chain that walks the prospect from "I believed Y was the cause" to "Z is the real cause"
- **Brand belief B4** (brand is trustworthy) — built incrementally through chain of accepted facts, never claimed directly

**Excludes / Avoid when**:
- **Most Aware** audiences who already accept the claim — don't gradualize what's already accepted (insulting + wasteful)
- Stage 1-2 markets where the claim itself is fresh and credible — Gradualization is overkill
- Short formats where there's no room to chain (very short ads, single-line subjects) — alternate structure or split across multiple touchpoints

**Wrong-technique warning** (Schwartz):
- *"If you violate your prospect's established beliefs in the slightest degree... nothing you promise him, no matter how appealing, can save your ad."*
- *"Most copywriters try to strengthen ads by piling promise upon promise. What they usually get for their troubles is greater sales resistance... One fully-believed promise has ten times the sales power of ten partially-believed promises."*

### 3.4 REDEFINITION

**Prospect symptom**:
A **specific objection** would block the sale if confronted directly. Schwartz names exactly three:

1. *"I can't handle this"* (fear of complexity)
2. *"Why would I need this?"* (low perceived importance)
3. *"That's too expensive"* (price-anchoring against the wrong reference)

**Activate when**:
- One of the three named objections is present and would block the sale
- The product is a breakthrough innovation generating natural resistance
- The prospect has tried and failed at the category before (Sherwin Cody example: prospects "absolutely convinced that good English was too hard for them")
- The objection would kill the sale if confronted but disappears if the frame is changed

**What it does**:
Reframes a built-in product handicap (or the prospect's existing concept of the category) by giving it a **new definition** that removes the objection — ideally before the prospect even realizes the objection existed.

**Method overview** — Schwartz names exactly three responses, one per objection:

- **"Too complicated"** → **Simplification**: break into tiny visible steps. "Just 3 steps." "15 minutes a day."
- **"Not important enough"** → **Escalation**: connect to bigger consequences. "This isn't about [small thing] — it's about [identity / future / consequence]."
- **"Too expensive"** → **Price Reduction by reframing**: switch the standard of comparison. Daily breakdown. ROI projection. Cost of NOT buying. Anchor against a different category.

> Price-reframing boundary: this section covers WHEN to deploy Redefinition; the operational catalog of price-reframing techniques (decomposition, dimensionalization, loss-anchoring, the stack) lives in [offer-construction](core/strategic-frameworks/offer-construction.md) §6 (+ §3.3).

Operational principle: redefinition is **surgical** — it addresses ONE objection precisely. Don't redefine generically.

**Position in copy**:
Inside the Gradualization structure — usually mid-body, after agreement has been built but before the goal-claim. The "turn" where the prospect's reality and the product's promise get joined.

**Stacks with**:
- **Gradualization** — lives inside it
- **Mechanization** — often needs proof to be believed: "Redefine the simplicity, then Mechanize why it's simple"
- **Concentration** — a redefinition can be of the alternatives ("their version is X, our version is Y because...")

**Typically installs (chain rings)** — see [chain-of-beliefs](core/strategic-frameworks/chain-of-beliefs.md):
- **Ring 4** (procrastination — affordability/risk objections):
  - #25 (I can afford this) — price reduction via reframing
  - #21 (risk is acceptable) — risk reversal via guarantee
  - #26 (mechanism guarantee) — when paired with UM
- **Ring 3** support: #14 (fits my life — when complexity objection arises)
- **Blocking belief dismantling** (chain-of-beliefs §8 hidden-cost technique) — when the blocking belief is true short-term but the cost reframe flips the math
- Activates surgically only where a NAMED objection (complexity / importance / price) is present

**Excludes / Avoid when**:
- No specific objection is present — Redefinition without a named obstacle is empty technique
- The objection is "not interested in the category" — that's mass-desire territory, not Redefinition

**Wrong-technique warning** (Schwartz):
- *"Innovation without acceptance is valueless. The more people know that something is difficult, and the more revolutionary your product is — the more resistance you will meet."*
- Shouting "EASY!" without redefining the field is ignored.
- Confronting the objection directly without redefining it ("just do your own TV repairs") kills the ad — the prospect rejects the premise.

**Key example from the book** (illustrative — not for imitation):
TV Repair Manual: "repairs" redefined as "adjustments"; "breakdowns" as "warning signals/symptoms" the manual teaches you to read. The prospect goes from "I can't do TV repair" to "I can read warning signals" — same task, different frame.

### 3.5 MECHANIZATION

**Prospect symptom**:
The prospect has accepted the promise and is silently asking: **"How does it work? Says who? Tell me more."** The promise is so strong it begins to sound "too good to be true." Schwartz's diagnostic: *"She needs reassurance fast."*

**Activate when**:
- A strong claim has just been made
- The promise sounds "too good to be true" without explanation
- A price cut needs a reason-why to be believed
- Sophistication Stage 3-4 markets where promises all sound alike — mechanism becomes the differentiator
- The prospect's "oh yeah?" reaction is anticipated at a specific point

**What it does**:
Verbally demonstrates **how** the product works — answers the prospect's anticipated *"How does it work?"* or *"Oh yeah?"* by showing the inner workings before the prospect has to ask aloud.

**Method overview** — depends on awareness/sophistication:

- **Mechanism known and accepted** (Stage 1 markets) → just **NAME** it (catalog-style copy)
- **Mechanism not understood** (Stage 2 markets) → **DESCRIBE** it (Promise + Reason Why)
- **Sophisticated market, all claims sound alike** (Stage 3) → **FEATURE** the mechanism, put it in the headline ("Shrinks Hemorrhoids Without Surgery" — the mechanism becomes the point of difference)
- **Mechanism is unfamiliar but desire is high** (Stage 4) → **ELABORATE** the mechanism with proof, analogies, stages

**Critical rule**: mechanism copy is NOT scientific discourse. Schwartz: *"Every word in good copy — including mechanism copy — sells."*

Use analogies, metaphors, sales language. When literal description would be dry, switch to vivid imagery that conveys the same mechanism. Mechanism is not a technical appendix — it's persuasion through demonstration.

**Position in copy**:
At **anticipation points** — wherever the prospect is silently asking "how?" or "oh yeah?" In sophisticated markets, mechanization moves on top of the ad (headline).

**Stacks with**:
- **Redefinition** — mechanizes the new definition (e.g., "this is why it's actually simple")
- **Concentration** — mechanizes the contrast (why your way works vs. theirs)
- **Gradualization** — each mechanism explanation is a step in the belief chain
- **Intensification** — mechanism + intensification = the prospect understands HOW and feels the result

**Typically installs (chain rings)** — see [chain-of-beliefs](core/strategic-frameworks/chain-of-beliefs.md):
- **Ring 2** (real cause is X — Problem Aware Route A): #5 (missing 1%), #6 (previous understanding wrong), #8 (past failures destined) — UMP explains the new cause
- **Ring 3** (Useful Mechanism for the problem): #9 (category works), #10 (addresses root cause), #13 (mechanism is clear), #17 (specific mechanism only this brand has), #18 (brand's UM) — UMS explains the how
- **Brand belief B2** (brand has the required competence) — mechanism articulation demonstrates competence
- **#24** (information is sufficient) — answering anticipation points satisfies the "tell me more" reflex
- **#26** (mechanism guarantee) — when mechanization is paired with risk reversal

**Excludes / Avoid when**:
- **Most Aware retargeting** — they already know how it works; mechanism wastes attention
- Before the prospect has accepted there's a problem — mechanism without context is noise
- Stage 1 markets where the claim itself is fresh — mechanism may dilute the promise

**Wrong-technique warning** (Schwartz):
- *"Mechanism copy is not scientific discourse. Never allow it to become dull, or merely factual."*
- Price cuts without mechanism / reason-why: *"What happened? No one believed them."*
- Naming the mechanism when the market doesn't yet understand it leaves the prospect cold; describing it when the market already knows wastes wordage.

### 3.6 CONCENTRATION

**Prospect symptom**:
- **Loyalty to an entrenched alternative** (uses a competitor by habit)
- **Habit-buying** in the category — never reconsidered
- Belief that the existing solution is "good enough" — comfortable, not seeking change

**Activate when** (Schwartz is precise — these are the conditions, not generic competitor work):

- Your advertising budget is **much less** than the competitor's (you can't out-broadcast — must out-position)
- The bulk of your prospects are **already customers** of the competitor — you must crack his image, shatter their loyalty, before you can re-channel their desire
- You have a clearly demonstrable point of contrast **AND** you can provide the solution at the same time

**When NOT to use** (Schwartz's exact contrary conditions):

- You **dominate the field** — protecting loyalty against challengers is better served by ignoring them ("don't give him the prestige of attack")
- Your story is "so powerful, so different, so fresh that the competition has nothing to match it" — don't mention competitors at all

**What it does**:
Direct attack — proves that **alternate ways** of satisfying the desire are ineffective, by contrasting their weaknesses with your product's strengths.

**Method overview**:
- **Attack mechanisms, NEVER brands.** Name what the competing approach fails to do; never name the competitor.
- **Point-by-point contrast** — the alternative's defect + your advantage in the same sentence (or parallel sentences)
- **Reframe the attack as service to the prospect** — "for his own good, in his service" — not as biased self-interest
- Use parallel bad/good structure or time-sequence (what's happening now → what happens when you switch)

**Position in copy**:
After desire and basic mechanism have been established. Sits inside the proof structure, often before the final close.

**Stacks with** (Concentration is itself a CONTAINER that uses the other techniques):
- **Intensification** — to dramatize the penalties of continuing with the old approach
- **Gradualization** — logical cause-effect chain leading to "therefore, switch"
- **Mechanization** — proves your solution's superiority within the contrast
- All deployed inside the bad/good structure

**Typically installs (chain rings)** — see [chain-of-beliefs](core/strategic-frameworks/chain-of-beliefs.md):
- **Ring 3** (specifically the differentiation beliefs): #11 (different from past failures), #22 (proposal superior to alternatives)
- **Ring 2** support: #8 (past failures destined to fail) — Concentration explains WHY the alternatives failed (their wrong mechanism)
- **Brand belief B3** (track record) — when the contrast is supported by the brand's accumulated results vs. competitors
- **Category-layer beliefs** (Solution Aware non-leader): when the prospect must reject the competing category, not just the competing brand

**Excludes / Avoid when**:
- You dominate the field (see above)
- Your story is incomparable (see above)
- You can attack but cannot show the solution side-by-side

**Wrong-technique warning** (Schwartz — this is the master rule of Concentration):
- *"**NEVER attack a weakness unless you can provide the solution to that weakness at the same time!**"*
- Pure attack without paired solution triggers *"skepticism and dislike, and very little else."*
- *"If you can only attack another product — without showing at the same time, by comparison, how your product provides what the other lacks — then say nothing at all!"*

### 3.7 CAMOUFLAGE

**Prospect symptom**:
**Instant and automatic skepticism** triggered by ad-style appearance. The prospect's conditioned reflex flags emotional / superlative / adjective-heavy copy as biased before the content is read. The "hard-sell stereotype" reaction.

Schwartz: *"Highly-emotional, adjective-heavy 'advertising language' tends to produce a counter-reaction in its prospects by its very appearance."*

**Activate when**:
- The medium has high editorial trust that can be transferred to the ad (news publications, respected blogs, professional newsletters)
- The category is **highly competitive** and standard ad voice has lost credibility from repetition
- Maximum believability is needed to overcome automatic skepticism
- The audience is sophisticated and has built defenses against typical ad-style language

**What it does**:
Borrows believability from the medium itself (or a non-ad genre) by mimicking its **format, phraseology, and tone** — bypasses the "this is an ad: beware!" reflex before content evaluation begins.

**Method overview** — Schwartz names 3 methods:

1. **Adopt FORMAT** — match the medium's specific cues. Typography, sub-head placement, illustration style, layout density. Example: Wall Street Journal-style ad with Journal typography, two-column layout, line drawings.

2. **Adopt PHRASEOLOGY** — match the genre's language conventions. News leads with dateline + by-line ("New York, N.Y. — A leading skin specialist today demonstrated..."). Trade journal vocabulary. Documentary tone.

3. **Adopt MOOD**:
   - **Understatement** — short, fall-toned sentences, few adjectives, calm voice. Volkswagen: "You never run out of air."
   - **Deadly Sincerity** — point out flaws openly so the benefits become more believable. "It is not meant for ninety-nine men out of every hundred." Self-restriction signals confidence.

**Position in copy**:
Operates at the level of **layout, voice, format, opening, and tone**. **Global, not positional** — wraps the entire piece. Decided before writing begins, then sustained throughout.

**Stacks with**:
- **Orthogonal to all other techniques** — compatible with everything. The 6 other techniques operate inside the camouflage voice.
- Particularly powerful with Identification (the borrowed voice carries identity implicitly) and with Mechanization (technical-feeling mechanism descriptions feel like editorial expertise).

**Typically installs (chain rings)** — see [chain-of-beliefs](core/strategic-frameworks/chain-of-beliefs.md):
- **Orthogonal — Camouflage doesn't install one specific ring.** It is the **voice layer** through which all other techniques deploy.
- Primary structural impact: **brand belief B4** (brand is trustworthy) — the borrowed-trust voice signals credibility before content is even evaluated
- **Brand belief B5** (brand cares — deadly sincerity variant): when self-restriction or transparent flaw-admission signals genuine relationship over selling intent
- **Blocking belief dismantling** (chain-of-beliefs §8 conflict-of-interest technique) — the third-party / outsider voice positions the writer as messenger, not seller, undermining the "they have a stake" defense

**Excludes / Avoid when**:
- Brands with strong native voice that audiences expect (changing the voice would feel inauthentic)
- Audiences without a learned defense against ad voice (audiences who already trust the brand — Camouflage is unnecessary)
- Direct-response formats where overt CTA / urgency is required (Camouflage can soften the call to a degree that loses conversion)

**Wrong-technique warning** (Schwartz):
- Faking the format superficially (without matching the medium's specific cues — subhead placement, type choices, illustration style) won't transfer the trust. The reader recognizes a fake ad-pretending-to-be-an-article and the trust collapses.
- The voice must be **sustained** — breaking into typical ad language mid-piece destroys what the opening built.

## 4. Sequencing logic — when each technique activates in the copy

Schwartz's Chapter 14 ("The Final Touches") prescribes a linear architecture. The techniques activate at specific structural points in this sequence:

```
1. HEADLINE
   ↓ built on State of Awareness + Sophistication
   ↓ tests for BOTH interest AND believability
   ↓ NO technique applies generically here — the headline is a strategy decision

2. LEAD (opens the body)
   ↓ GRADUALIZATION opens with the first acceptances:
   ↓   - Inclusion questions (yes-questions)
   ↓   - Detailed identification (recognized symptoms)
   ↓   - Contradiction of false beliefs (gently)

3. BODY (main argument)
   ↓ INTENSIFICATION drives desire forward
   ↓ IDENTIFICATION weaves role through illustration and mood
   ↓ Both run in parallel through every body section

4. ANTICIPATION POINTS (the prospect's silent questions)
   ↓ "How does it work?" → MECHANIZATION enters
   ↓ "Oh yeah? Says who?" → MECHANIZATION + verification

5. OBJECTION POINTS (specific resistances surface)
   ↓ "Too complicated" / "Not important enough" / "Too expensive" → REDEFINITION reframes

6. COMPETITOR LOYALTY (if applicable)
   ↓ CONCENTRATION attacks — only with paired solution

7. VERIFICATION (proof deployment)
   ↓ Interwoven where the prospect is BEGGING for it (never in a "proof box")
   ↓ Inside Mechanization steps, inside Concentration contrasts, inside Redefinition turns

8. CLOSE
   ↓ INTENSIFICATION can return as final image-summary
   ↓ Guarantee as final intensification of desire

9. THE WHOLE WRAPPED IN CAMOUFLAGE (if appropriate)
   ↓ Voice / format / tone chosen at the architecture level
   ↓ Sustained through every paragraph
```

The sequencing means: **techniques are NOT applied uniformly across the piece.** They activate at specific structural moments, in response to specific prospect states.

## 5. Stacking rules — what coexists, what contains what

The techniques are not all peers. Some are containers; some are atomic; one is orthogonal.

| Relationship | Description |
|---|---|
| **Intensification + Identification** | Stack always. Operate on different dimensions (desire vs. expression). Both can run continuously throughout the body without conflict. |
| **Gradualization as container** | Gradualization is the **structural architecture** of the piece. Redefinition, Mechanization, and Concentration all sit INSIDE the gradualization chain — each one is a chain step that handles a specific obstacle. |
| **Concentration as container** | Concentration deploys Intensification (penalties of continuing with old), Gradualization (logical cause-effect chain), and Mechanization (proof of solution) inside its bad/good contrasts. |
| **Camouflage as orthogonal** | Operates on voice and format only. Compatible with every other technique. Wraps the piece — the other 6 happen INSIDE the camouflaged voice. |
| **Mechanization on demand** | Activates at specific anticipation points, not continuously. Returns to silence after answering the implicit question. |
| **Redefinition surgical** | Activates ONLY when a named objection (complexity / importance / price) is present. Surgical, not generic. |

**Implication for the AI**: a piece can have Intensification + Identification + Gradualization + (Mechanization at one moment) + (Redefinition at one moment) + Camouflage running simultaneously. This is normal. What is NOT normal is forcing all 7 techniques into every paragraph.

## 6. Mutual exclusions — when one technique excludes another

Schwartz is explicit about cases where techniques cannot coexist:

### 6.1 Concentration excludes itself when you dominate

If you are the market leader, attacking smaller competitors **gives them prestige** and signals insecurity. The right move is to ignore them entirely. Concentration is a challenger's technique, not a leader's.

### 6.2 Identification cannot contradict the primary image

You cannot project an identity that the prospect's accepted images would reject. A mass-market product trying to project elite identity, an industrial product trying to project luxury — both fail because *"the disbelief spread to the performance claims themselves, and killed the sales."* Identity must build on what's accepted, not replace it.

### 6.3 Power-claim Intensification in the headline excludes itself when belief barriers > desire barriers

If the prospect would reject the strongest claim (because of pre-existing beliefs), putting it in the headline kills the piece before Intensification can do its work. Gradualization must open instead — the headline operates on belief-readiness, not desire-amplification.

### 6.4 Mechanization on Most Aware audiences

Most Aware audiences know how the product works. Re-mechanizing for them is condescending and wasteful — they want offer + urgency, not explanation.

### 6.5 Camouflage excludes when the brand has strong native voice

A brand with a recognized voice that audiences expect cannot suddenly mimic editorial format — the inauthenticity breaks trust faster than ad-style would have. Camouflage is for brands whose native voice doesn't yet exist or doesn't fit the medium.

### 6.6 Generic over-application

Schwartz's overarching exclusion: applying any technique without a corresponding prospect symptom is itself a wrong-technique error. Empty technique reads as manipulation and produces resistance.

## 7. Self-diagnostic protocol — how the writer uses this file

The diagnostic decision tree is consulted DURING writing (when stuck) and AFTER drafting (when revising). It is NOT consulted as a pre-writing checklist.

### 7.1 During writing — when stuck

The writer is mid-section and the copy feels weak. Open this file and ask:

1. **What is the prospect feeling at THIS point in the copy?**
   - Vague longing without compulsion → Intensification missing
   - Skepticism about a claim just made → Mechanization missing
   - Resistance from a pre-existing belief → Gradualization broken (the previous step was too big)
   - A specific objection (too complex / not important / too expensive) surfacing → Redefinition needed
   - Loyalty to a competitor unaddressed → Concentration needed
   - The voice feels overtly ad-like and the audience would reject it → Camouflage needed
   - The product feels generic and the prospect doesn't see himself in it → Identification missing

2. **Match the symptom to the technique.**

3. **Deploy that technique only.** Do not deploy others "while you're at it."

### 7.2 After drafting — revision pass

Read the draft section by section. For each section, ask:

1. *"What is the prospect feeling here?"*
2. *"Is the matching technique present?"*
3. *"Is there a symptom present that has no technique responding to it?"*
4. *"Is there a technique present that has no symptom to respond to?"*

The second-to-last question identifies **missing responses** — add them.
The last question identifies **empty technique** — cut it.

### 7.3 The three antidotes to over-application

1. **"If you can't articulate the prospect's symptom that this technique is responding to, DON'T add it."** Empty technique reads as manipulation.

2. **"If the technique is already implicit in the writing, don't label it."** Schwartz's example of a piece that intensifies through one vivid image without ever announcing "now let me intensify" is the gold standard. Implicit > explicit.

3. **"Better one fully-deployed technique than five partial ones."** Schwartz: *"One fully-believed promise has ten times the sales power of ten partially-believed promises."* The same applies to techniques.

### 7.4 The "no technique" answer

A section can be technique-free. Schwartz never says every paragraph must deploy a technique. Some sections simply present information, set up the next step, or let the prospect breathe between persuasion-loaded moments. If the answer to *"what technique is responding to what symptom?"* is *"no symptom present, no technique needed,"* that is a valid answer.

The over-application failure mode is treating "no technique" as a failure of analysis. It isn't. It's the most common correct answer in well-paced copy.

---

## 8. Master rule (Schwartz)

> *"Belief depends on structure. Desire depends on promise. Every choice in copy is a choice about preparation: what does this prospect already accept, and what's the next step he can take without breaking?"*

The 7 techniques are the 7 forms of preparation Schwartz catalogued. Each one prepares the prospect for a specific next step that he could not have taken raw.

When in doubt about which technique applies, return to this rule. Identify what the prospect already accepts. Identify the next step he is being asked to take. Ask: *"Will this step break him?"* If yes, find the technique that prepares him. If no, no technique is needed.

## 10. What this file is NOT

To prevent the failure mode of the previous Copy Genius writing system:

- **NOT a checklist** — the techniques are not all to be applied to every piece
- **NOT a toolbox to deploy uniformly** — each technique has a specific trigger
- **NOT a list of sub-methods to apply per paragraph** — Schwartz's "13 methods of intensification" are NOT to be checked off; they are angles available IF intensification is the right response
- **NOT a substitute for the brand's voice** — voice (from `brand-copy-rules.md` and `brand.md`) is the dominant layer; techniques operate inside the voice, never above it
- **NOT to be read before writing as preparation BY THE WRITING SPECIALISTS** (consumer b) — they read it DURING writing when stuck or AFTER drafting for diagnosis. This prohibition does NOT apply to the Strategist (consumer a), who reads §3 at brief time to pre-assign techniques — that is assignment work on the brief, not pre-writing preparation of a draft.
- **NOT all equal in importance for every piece** — the brief's awareness level and sophistication stage determine which 2-3 techniques will dominate; the others stay quiet

The single biggest mistake in copy writing is treating the 7 techniques as parallel methods to deploy in every paragraph. They are sequential responses to sequential prospect states. **Diagnose, then deploy.**
